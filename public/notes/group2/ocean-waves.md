Most surface waves transfer energy supplied by wind. Wave growth depends on wind speed, duration and fetch, the uninterrupted distance over water. Wavelength measures crest-to-crest distance. Period is the time between successive crests at a fixed location. Individual water particles often follow oscillatory paths while the wave form travels.

In shallow water, the seabed slows waves. As crests approach the coast at an angle, the shallow part slows first and the crest turns toward alignment with the shore. Breaking waves move sediment. Oblique swash and downslope backwash can transport it along a beach by longshore drift. Tsunamis are long waves generated by sudden displacement of water.

## Worked example

A storm blows for many hours across an unobstructed stretch of ocean. The strong wind, sustained duration and long fetch are all favourable for large wind waves.

## Common mistake

Tsunamis owe their origin to water displacement, often from a submarine earthquake. Ordinary wind waves get their energy from surface winds.

## Practice

How can angled waves build a spit?

**Answer:** Longshore drift transports sediment along the coast. Where the Shoreline turns or flow conditions change, some sediment accumulates outward from land.
