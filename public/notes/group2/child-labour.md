Child labour can interrupt schooling, threaten health and reinforce poverty. The Child and Adolescent Labour Act generally prohibits employment of children under 14, subject to statutory exceptions, and prohibits adolescents aged 14 to under 18 from specified hazardous occupations and processes. Article 24 bars employment of children below 14 in factories, mines and other hazardous employment. Education, household income support and inspection can address different causes.

## Worked example

An item describes a 16-year-old working in a prohibited hazardous process. The adolescent restriction applies. If the person were under 14, the broader child employment restrictions would also need consideration.

## Common mistake

Age definitions differ across statutes. For labour questions distinguish the child and adolescent bands used by the applicable Act.

## Practice

Why can school enrolment alone miss child labour?

**Answer:** A child may be enrolled while working outside school hours, experiencing irregular attendance or leaving before completion.
