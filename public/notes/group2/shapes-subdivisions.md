For counting figures, organize by size and orientation. For a rectangular grid, selecting two vertical and two horizontal grid lines defines exactly one rectangle. Count squares separately by side length. When diagonals appear, trace the permitted edges and mark counted shapes.

## Concepts in depth

Distinguish equal areas from equal shapes, and count full figures separately from partial pieces. Under a line crossing a grid, an internal corner crossing can enter the next cell without crossing two distinct boundaries. Symmetry can simplify a repeated subdivision.

## How to apply it

One diagonal splits a rectangle into two equal areas. In an m by n rectangular grid with endpoints at opposite corners, a diagonal crosses m+n−gcd(m,n) cells. Derive it by subtracting duplicated boundary counts at common intersections.

## Worked example

A 2 × 3 grid has 3 horizontal and 4 vertical lines. Choose 2 each to count 3 × 6 = 18 rectangles, including squares.

## Common mistake

A shape assembled from several small cells counts if its complete outline is present. Avoid counting only the smallest components.

## Practice

How many axis-aligned squares are in a 2 × 3 unit grid?

**Answer:** Eight. Six 1 × 1 squares and two 2 × 2 squares.
