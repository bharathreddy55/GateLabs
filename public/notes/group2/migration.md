Migration is a change of usual residence across a defined boundary. Origin and destination determine whether a flow is rural–rural, rural–urban, urban–urban or urban–rural. Indian census migration tables distinguish measures such as place of birth and place of last residence. Employment, education, marriage, family movement and environment shape different streams. Andhra Pradesh has seasonal labour movement as well as longer-term movement to towns and other states.

## Concepts in depth

Migration can be internal or international, seasonal or more permanent. Push and pull factors often interact, and migration decisions depend on resources and social networks. Net figures can hide large flows in both directions.

## How to apply it

If 1,200 enter and 900 leave, net in-migration is 300, while gross movements total 2,100. Remittances can be economically significant even when a move is seasonal. Read which definition of migrant an official table uses.

## Worked example

A person born in Kurnool lived in Vijayawada and then settled in Visakhapatnam. Place-of-birth data describe Kurnool as the origin, while last-residence data describe Vijayawada.

## Common mistake

Distinguish recent migration flows from the stock of residents who have ever moved. A change of the measurement definition changes the answer.

## Practice

How is net migration calculated?

**Answer:** Subtract out-migrants from in-migrants for the same area and period.
