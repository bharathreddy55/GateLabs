Chandragupta established the Mauryan dynasty in the late fourth century BCE after the Nandas. Bindusara succeeded him, and Ashoka's inscriptions record policies across much of the subcontinent. Pataliputra was the imperial capital. Inscriptions and archaeology suggest a network of political centres and varying degrees of imperial control across regions.

Agriculture supported revenue, and roads, markets and craft communities sustained exchange. Ashoka appointed dhamma-mahamattas to promote public ethical conduct and welfare. His message included restraint, concern for living beings and respect for different sects. Pillar capitals, rock-cut Barabar caves and early stupa patronage illustrate distinct forms of Mauryan art. The Arthashastra preserves ideas about statecraft and revenue in a composite text. Read Ashoka's edicts as direct evidence of the policies he proclaimed.

## Concepts in depth

Chandragupta established the dynasty, followed by Bindusara and Ashoka. Ashokan inscriptions convey Dhamma and administrative communications across regions. Regional variations in script or language matter. The Arthashastra is traditionally attributed to Kautilya, but its composition history differs from that of contemporary inscriptions.

## How to apply it

Read inscriptions as evidence of intended policy and communication. Major Rock Edict XIII is associated with remorse after Kalinga. Compare an inscription, an external account and a later textual tradition before treating them as equivalent sources.

## Worked example

Major Rock Edict XIII refers to suffering caused by the conquest of Kalinga and presents conquest through dhamma as preferable. Match this clue to Ashoka and to a royal inscription.

## Common mistake

Avoid assuming every practice discussed in the Arthashastra was applied uniformly across Ashoka's empire.

## Quick revision

Pataliputra was the capital. Kalinga figures in Major Rock Edict XIII. Barabar caves preserve early rock-cut architecture.

## Practice

Which source directly records Ashoka's response to the Kalinga war?

**Answer:** Major Rock Edict XIII, an inscription issued by the ruler himself.
