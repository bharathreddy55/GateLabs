Marriage establishes socially or legally recognized relationships, and practices vary across communities and time. Endogamy favours marriage within a defined group. Exogamy favours marriage outside a specified group. Both may operate together at different levels. Monogamy, polygyny and polyandry describe different arrangements. Post-marital residence can be patrilocal, matrilocal or neolocal.

## Concepts in depth

Marriage regulates recognised partnership, kinship and obligations in different ways. Endogamy involves marriage within a defined group and exogamy outside it. Residence after marriage and the descent system describe different aspects of social organisation.

## How to apply it

Marriage can be endogamous by caste and exogamous by clan simultaneously because the boundaries differ. Patrilocal residence concerns where partners live. It does not by itself establish how inheritance or descent is traced.

## Worked example

A community expects a person to marry within their caste and outside their own clan. The first expectation is caste endogamy and the second is clan exogamy.

## Common mistake

Always identify the group boundary before deciding whether a rule is endogamous or exogamous.

## Practice

What does neolocal residence mean?

**Answer:** A couple establishes a new household apart from both partners' parental households.
