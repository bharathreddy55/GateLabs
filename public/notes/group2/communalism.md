In Indian social studies, communalism refers to mobilizing religious identity around claims that religious communities have inherently conflicting social or political interests. Propaganda, segregation and political incentives can intensify polarization. Historical analysis distinguishes long-term institutional conditions, organizing strategies and immediate incidents.

## Concepts in depth

Communalism converts a religious identity into an exclusive political or social “us versus them” framework. Rumours, stereotypes and uneven mobilisation can escalate tension. Religious observance and inter-group differences do not themselves establish communalism.

## How to apply it

Distinguish a religion-based festival from a campaign depicting another faith community as collectively threatening. Analyse how institutions, leadership, local triggers and misinformation can mediate outcomes rather than treating conflict as inevitable.

## Worked example

A neighbourhood celebration brings people together for worship. A separate campaign portrays another religious community as a collective enemy to gain political support. The second scenario shows communal mobilization.

## Common mistake

Treat religious diversity and communal conflict as distinct concepts. Look for the mechanism turning identity into perceived opposition.

## Practice

How can misinformation intensify communal tension?

**Answer:** False or decontextualized reports can assign collective blame, accelerate fear and provoke retaliation before facts are checked.
