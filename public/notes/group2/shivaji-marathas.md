Shivaji built a kingdom in the western Deccan from a network of forts, mobile forces and local alliances. The terrain of the Sahyadris helped fort defence and rapid troop movement. His conflicts with Bijapur and the Mughals included the Treaty of Purandar in 1665. He was crowned at Raigad in 1674 and supported a naval presence along the western coast.

The Ashtapradhan comprised eight senior offices. The Peshwa was a chief minister in Shivaji's administration and later became more powerful under his successors. Chauth was a quarter levy, and sardeshmukhi referred to an additional claim commonly described as one tenth. In the eighteenth century, Peshwas and regional Maratha houses extended power far beyond the original kingdom. The 1761 defeat at Panipat occurred in this later phase.

## Concepts in depth

Shivaji was crowned in 1674 at Raigad. Forts, flexible military organisation and administration supported consolidation. The Ashta Pradhan council belongs to his governing arrangements. Maratha power later evolved under Peshwas and confederate houses.

## How to apply it

Chauth conventionally refers to a one-fourth levy and sardeshmukhi to an additional one-tenth claim. Match them to fiscal arrangements rather than court offices. Distinguish Shivaji's seventeenth-century state from the later confederacy.

## Worked example

An item links Raigad and 1674 with an assertion of sovereign rule. The event is Shivaji's coronation. A question linking Peshwa dominance and Panipat in 1761 refers to later Maratha history.

## Common mistake

Separate Shivaji's administrative institutions from the eighteenth-century political arrangements of the Maratha confederacy.

## Quick revision

Raigad → 1674 coronation. Ashtapradhan → eight senior offices. Chauth → one quarter.

## Practice

Why did hill forts matter to Shivaji's state?

**Answer:** They defended routes, stored supplies and gave mobile forces secure bases in difficult terrain.
