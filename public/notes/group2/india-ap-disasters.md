A hazard is a potentially damaging event or process. Exposure describes people and assets in harm's way, and vulnerability describes their susceptibility to loss. Disaster risk also depends on capacity to prepare and respond. Mitigation reduces risk before events, preparedness organizes early warnings and action, response addresses immediate needs, and recovery restores services and livelihoods.

In India, examples include Himalayan earthquakes and landslides, river floods, heat waves, drought and coastal cyclones. Andhra Pradesh faces Bay of Bengal cyclones and storm surge, flooding in rivers and low-lying settlements, and recurring heat and rainfall deficits in the interior. Forecasts from IMD and local alerts should lead to specific decisions such as evacuation of vulnerable coastal areas, emergency shelter operations or heat action measures. Urban flood planning includes drainage capacity, floodplain management and maintenance.

## Worked example

A cyclone warning predicts high surge for a low-lying village. Moving residents to designated higher shelters is preparedness and immediate protective action. Building shelters and restricting critical infrastructure in surge zones reduce long-term risk.

## Common mistake

Storm surge is abnormal sea-level rise driven by a storm, with actual coastal inundation also affected by the tide, waves and local topography.

## Practice

Why can storms of comparable wind intensity cause different losses?

**Answer:** Exposure, surge, rainfall, local geography, building strength, warning lead time and evacuation capacity differ.
