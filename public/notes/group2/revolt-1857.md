The uprising began among Bengal Army sepoys and spread from Meerut to Delhi in May 1857. The rumour that rifle cartridges were greased with cow and pig fat sharpened anxieties over religion. Older grievances included annexation, military service conditions and landed interests, especially in Awadh. The groups who joined varied by region and had different objectives.

Bahadur Shah Zafar became a symbolic leader in Delhi. Nana Sahib was associated with Kanpur, Begum Hazrat Mahal with Lucknow, Rani Lakshmibai with Jhansi and Kunwar Singh with Bihar. British forces recaptured major centres, and the uprising was largely suppressed by 1858. The Government of India Act 1858 transferred Company rule to the Crown. The army was reorganized, and relations with princely rulers changed.

## Worked example

A question gives Lucknow and organized resistance after the annexation of Awadh. Match these clues to Begum Hazrat Mahal, who supported her son's claim and helped lead resistance.

## Common mistake

Treat the cartridge controversy as a trigger. An explanation of the spread of revolt also needs regional political and economic grievances.

## Practice

What institutional change followed the uprising?

**Answer:** The Crown took over government from the East India Company in 1858, altering the formal authority responsible for governing British India.
