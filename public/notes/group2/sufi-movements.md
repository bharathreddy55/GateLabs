Sufism encompasses Islamic traditions of spiritual discipline and devotion. Sufi lineages, called silsilas, connected disciples with teachers. The khanqah served as a lodge for teaching, hospitality and community life. A dargah is a shrine associated with a saint's tomb. Chishti figures include Muin al-Din Chishti of Ajmer and Nizamuddin Auliya of Delhi. The Suhrawardi order had important centres including Multan.

Spiritual practices could include remembrance of God, service and, in some circles, devotional listening or sama. Relations to rulers varied among orders and individuals. Textual evidence includes biographies, collections of conversations and poetry. Compare institutional forms and devotional practices with their local settings, especially when an exam question associates individuals with sites.

## Concepts in depth

Sufi orders or silsilas transmitted spiritual teaching through lineages. Khanqahs provided spaces for instruction and hospitality, and dargahs developed around shrines. Chishti and Suhrawardi orders differed in settings and relationships with patrons.

## How to apply it

Associate Muinuddin Chishti with Ajmer and Nizamuddin Auliya with Delhi. Sama is devotional listening in some practices. Neither one order's relation to rulers nor use of music automatically applies to all Sufi communities.

## Worked example

A question describes visitors gathering at the tomb of Muin al-Din Chishti. The site is the Ajmer dargah. A living master's lodge would be described as a khanqah.

## Common mistake

Keep the teacher's order, location and type of institution separate in matching questions.

## Quick revision

Silsila → lineage. Khanqah → lodge. Dargah → shrine. Muin al-Din Chishti → Ajmer.

## Practice

What was the usual function of a khanqah?

**Answer:** It provided a place for spiritual instruction, gathering, hospitality and service under a Sufi teacher.
