Identify a property shared by all but one candidate. Check mathematical type, spelling, category and relationship before choosing. The best exam answer generally uses a simple property that cleanly separates the options.

## Concepts in depth

Propose a shared rule before picking the unusual-looking item. For numbers compare factorisation, powers, digit operations and divisibility. For word or figure questions compare a common relation or invariant. The intended rule should explain all remaining items.

## How to apply it

27, 64, 125 and 200 contain three consecutive cubes. Since 200 is not a cube, it breaks the strongest common rule. An alternative such as “not divisible by 3” fits several terms but leaves more than one exception.

## Worked example

9, 16, 25, 27, 36. Four numbers are perfect squares. 27 is a cube and is the only term that fails the square test.

## Common mistake

Choose a distinction that classifies all other members together. A property shared by only two of them is weaker.

## Practice

Which is odd among cuboid, cube, sphere and square?

**Answer:** Square. The other three are three-dimensional solids.
