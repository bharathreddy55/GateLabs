Endogenic processes such as uplift and volcanism build or deform relief. Weathering breaks down rock in place. Mass movement carries it downslope under gravity, and rivers, glaciers, wind and sea waves erode, transport and deposit material. A landform often reflects several stages of these processes.

Rivers cut relatively narrow V-shaped valleys where downcutting dominates. In gentler reaches, water erodes the outside of meander bends and deposits on the inside. A cutoff may leave an oxbow lake. Glaciers can broaden valleys into U-shaped troughs and deposit moraines. Wind can form sand dunes through deposition. Waves erode cliffs and can transport sediment alongshore to build beaches or spits. Limestone dissolves in weakly acidic water, producing karst caves and sinkholes.

## Concepts in depth

Internal processes build relief through tectonics and volcanism. External processes weather, erode, transport and deposit material. Rivers, glaciers, winds and waves have characteristic erosional and depositional forms, though local geology controls details.

## How to apply it

A meander cut-off can produce an oxbow lake, while a river's sediment near its mouth can form a delta under suitable conditions. Match V-shaped valleys to fluvial incision and common U-shaped valleys to glacial erosion.

## Worked example

A map shows a crescent-shaped lake near a meandering river. Reconstruct the sequence: the bend neck narrows, flood flow cuts a straighter channel, and sediment seals the old bend.

## Common mistake

Separate the erosion and deposition clues. A glacial trough indicates erosion, while a moraine records deposited glacial debris.

## Practice

Which process produces a point bar on the inner side of a river bend?

**Answer:** Deposition, where slower flow along the inner bend can drop sediment.
