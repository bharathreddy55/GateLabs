Vasco da Gama reached Calicut by sea in 1498. The Portuguese developed an armed trading network, established Goa as a major base and used the cartaz pass system. Dutch, English and French companies followed in different periods. The Dutch valued textile and spice networks and operated at places such as Pulicat. The English East India Company obtained a charter in 1600, and the French company was founded in 1664.

The companies sought pepper, textiles and other trade goods. Control of ports, access to inland producers and arrangements with local rulers mattered as much as ship arrivals. On the Coromandel coast, Masulipatnam and Pulicat connected maritime merchants with textile production. English and French rivalry in the Carnatic later became intertwined with regional succession disputes.

## Worked example

A question describes a permit controlling Indian Ocean shipping under Portuguese power. The clue is cartaz. If it describes the English company's foundation, use the charter date of 1600.

## Common mistake

A European factory was a trading station with warehouses and agents. Read the term in its historical commercial sense.

## Quick revision

1498 → da Gama at Calicut. Goa → Portuguese base. Cartaz → maritime pass. Masulipatnam and Pulicat → Coromandel commerce.

## Practice

Why did Europeans seek Coromandel textile ports?

**Answer:** They connected companies with producers of cloth for Asian and European markets, giving companies a valuable exchange commodity.
