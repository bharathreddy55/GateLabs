Tides are periodic changes in sea level caused mainly by the differences in gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun across Earth, together with the motion of the Earth–Moon system. Many coasts see two high tides and two low tides per lunar day, while basin geometry creates mixed and diurnal regimes elsewhere.

Around new and full moons, the Sun, Earth and Moon align and the tidal range increases. These are spring tides. Around the first and third quarter moons, the solar and lunar tidal effects partly offset, reducing the range in a neap tide. Local depth, coastline shape, friction and resonances affect the timing and height observed at a harbour.

## Worked example

If high water is 4.8 m and low water is 1.1 m relative to the same datum, the tidal range is 4.8 − 1.1 = 3.7 m.

## Common mistake

The term spring tide describes a large tidal range and applies in every season.

## Practice

What lunar phases are associated with neap tides?

**Answer:** The first and third quarters, when the directions of the Sun and Moon as seen from Earth are approximately at right angles.
