Babur's victory at Panipat in 1526 established Mughal power in northern India. Humayun lost it temporarily to the Sur rulers and returned in 1555. Akbar built a durable imperial administration through alliances, military expansion and revenue reform. Mansabdars held rank, expressed through zat and sawar, and often received jagirs whose revenue supported their obligations. The zabt system used measurement and assessed rates in areas where such administration was practical.

Agriculture generated much of state revenue, while textiles and other crafts connected towns with long-distance trade. Akbar's sulh-i kul expressed a policy of broad tolerance in governance. Persian chronicles such as the Akbarnama and Ain-i Akbari record court history and institutions. Translation projects brought Sanskrit works into Persian, including the Mahabharata as the Razmnama. Mughal painting developed under imperial patronage. Fatehpur Sikri, Humayun's Tomb and the Taj Mahal illustrate different architectural phases.

## Concepts in depth

A mansab specified rank with zat for personal status and sawar tied to cavalry obligations, with historical evolution. A jagir assigned revenue rather than outright private ownership. Under Akbar, Todar Mal is associated with systematic revenue assessment and zabt in suitable areas.

## How to apply it

Match the Ain-i-Akbari with Abul Fazl and the reign of Akbar. Distinguish a fiscal assessment method from a military obligation. Mansab holders might be transferred, which matters when judging how the centre attempted to control assigned resources.

## Worked example

A question says an officer's rank includes personal status and an obligation to maintain cavalry. These are zat and sawar within mansabdari. A jagir is a revenue assignment used to support payment.

## Common mistake

Distinguish a mansab, which is an assigned rank, from a jagir, which is a revenue assignment.

## Quick revision

Akbar → consolidated administration. Abu'l Fazl → Akbarnama and Ain-i Akbari. Razmnama → Persian Mahabharata.

## Practice

Which chronicle documents Akbar's administrative arrangements in detail?

**Answer:** The Ain-i Akbari by Abu'l Fazl, the administrative and statistical part of the Akbarnama project.
