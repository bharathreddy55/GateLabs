Abduction chooses the best available explanation for observed evidence. Compare how well each explanation accounts for all clues, what background assumptions it needs, and what evidence would discriminate between rivals. The result is defeasible when new evidence arrives.

## Worked example

Several houses on the same street lose power at once. A fault affecting their shared distribution line explains the clustering better than unrelated appliance faults.

## Common mistake

A good explanation is plausible given current clues. Keep alternatives available if the evidence is compatible with them.

## Practice

Roads are wet throughout a neighbourhood just after clouds clear. What explanation is plausible and what would corroborate it?

**Answer:** Recent rain is plausible. Rain gauge readings or radar observations could strengthen or weaken that explanation.
