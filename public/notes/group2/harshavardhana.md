Harshavardhana ruled from 606 to 647 CE, first associated with Thanesar and later with Kannauj. Our accounts include Banabhatta's Harshacharita, Xuanzang's travel record and Harsha's inscriptions. He extended influence in northern India. Pulakeshin II of the Chalukyas checked his advance near the Narmada, an episode associated with the Aihole inscription.

Harsha patronized scholars and religious institutions. Xuanzang describes religious assemblies and royal giving. Ratnavali, Priyadarsika and Nagananda are traditionally attributed to Harsha. Royal charters, including the Banskhera and Madhuban grants, help reconstruct administration and land patronage. Use the biography, travel account and inscriptions together because their authors wrote for different audiences.

## Worked example

An exam item mentions a Chinese traveller at Kannauj who also visited Nalanda. Xuanzang fits Harsha's period. Faxian is associated with the earlier Gupta period.

## Common mistake

Keep Harsha's northern power distinct from control over the Deccan. The Narmada encounter helps locate the limit of his southern advance.

## Quick revision

Banabhatta wrote Harshacharita. Xuanzang visited during Harsha's reign. Pulakeshin II checked the advance near the Narmada.

## Practice

Which author wrote the Harshacharita?

**Answer:** Banabhatta, a court author whose literary biography presents Harsha favourably.
