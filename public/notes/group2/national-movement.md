The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. Early leaders used petitions and economic criticism. Dadabhai Naoroji's drain argument traced the transfer of resources out of India. The 1905 partition of Bengal stimulated Swadeshi and boycott. The Home Rule leagues led by Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Annie Besant in 1916 sought self-government, with Besant particularly active from Madras.

Gandhi's Champaran campaign in 1917 addressed indigo cultivators' grievances. Non-Cooperation began in 1920 and was withdrawn in 1922. The 1930 Salt March opened a major phase of Civil Disobedience. In Andhra, Duggirala Gopalakrishnayya was associated with the Chirala–Perala movement, and Alluri Sitarama Raju led the 1922–24 Rampa rebellion. Quit India began in 1942. Subhas Chandra Bose led the Indian National Army in its later phase. These mass campaigns, local movements, wartime changes and negotiations formed overlapping strands before independence and partition in 1947.

## Concepts in depth

Track early Congress politics, Swadeshi, Gandhian mass movements and final negotiations separately. Non-Cooperation (1920), Civil Disobedience (1930) and Quit India (1942) used different contexts and demands. Peasant, worker, women and other groups contributed through different channels.

## How to apply it

A chronology question may place the 1929 Purna Swaraj resolution before the Salt March, and the Cabinet Mission in 1946 after Quit India. A movement's stated goals should be distinguished from the immediate outcome of suspending or ending it.

## Worked example

Put Civil Disobedience, Swadeshi, Quit India and Non-Cooperation in order. Use 1905 → 1920 → 1930 → 1942.

## Common mistake

Keep the Rampa rebellion distinct from the Gandhian strategy of nonviolent non-cooperation, even though they overlapped in time.

## Practice

Why was salt chosen in 1930?

**Answer:** The tax and monopoly touched a basic everyday necessity, making salt a widely understood issue around which to mobilize civil disobedience.
