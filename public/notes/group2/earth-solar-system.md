Earth is the third planet from the Sun and one of the four rocky planets. Its rotation produces day and night and determines the relationship between longitude and local solar time. Earth rotates once relative to the Sun in about 24 hours, so 15° of longitude corresponds to about one hour. Places farther east see local solar noon earlier in absolute time.

Earth's tilted rotation axis, about 23.4° from a line perpendicular to its orbital plane, changes the angle of sunlight and day length through the year. During the June solstice, the Northern Hemisphere tilts toward the Sun. Around the equinoxes, the subsolar point is near the equator. At a solar eclipse, the Moon lies between Earth and the Sun. At a lunar eclipse, Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.

## Concepts in depth

Earth rotates and revolves concurrently. Seasons primarily follow axial tilt and changing angle and duration of sunlight, not yearly variation in distance from the Sun. Lunar phases depend on viewing the illuminated half. Eclipses require closer alignment near orbital nodes.

## How to apply it

A solar eclipse can occur at new Moon and a lunar eclipse at full Moon, but not every month because the Moon's orbit is inclined. Solstices correspond to extreme seasonal solar declination, while equinoxes are near equal day and night globally.

## Worked example

If local solar time is noon at 75° E, what time is it at 90° E? The second place is 15° farther east, so its local solar time is about 1 p.m.

## Common mistake

Earth is near perihelion in early January. The seasons arise mainly from axial tilt and the resulting differences in day length and solar angle.

## Practice

Why is there no eclipse at every new or full Moon?

**Answer:** The Moon's orbital plane is tilted relative to Earth's orbit. An eclipse requires the bodies to align near a point where those planes intersect.
