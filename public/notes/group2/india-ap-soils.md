Soils form through interactions among parent material, climate, organisms, topography and time. Indian soil groups commonly studied for exams include alluvial, black, red and yellow, laterite, arid, forest and saline soils. Alluvial soils occur in river plains and deltas. Black soils are rich in clay and shrink and swell with changing moisture. Red colour commonly reflects iron oxides. Intense leaching under suitable warm and wet conditions helps create lateritic soils.

In Andhra Pradesh, alluvial soils support intensively cultivated delta plains. Red soils are widespread in parts of the uplands and Rayalaseema. Black soils occur in pockets, including parts of interior valleys and plains. Coastal and irrigated tracts can experience salinity or waterlogging where drainage is inadequate. Crop suitability also depends on slope, water supply, depth and soil management.

## Worked example

Deep clay develops wide cracks in the dry season and swells after rain. This shrink–swell behaviour points to a black soil with expansive clay.

## Common mistake

Red colour indicates oxidized iron and tells little by itself about a soil's available nutrients or productivity.

## Practice

How can improved drainage help a waterlogged irrigated field?

**Answer:** It removes excess water from the root zone and can help prevent salts from accumulating where evaporation is high.
