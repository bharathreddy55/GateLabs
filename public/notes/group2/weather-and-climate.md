Weather is the state of the atmosphere at a particular time and place. Climate describes long-term patterns and variability in temperature, rainfall and circulation. Latitude governs the usual solar angle. Altitude, ocean influence, prevailing winds, relief and currents also shape regional patterns.

Air moves under pressure gradients and is deflected by Earth's rotation. Rising air expands and cools. If it reaches saturation, condensation can produce clouds and rain. Orographic rainfall occurs when moist air rises over relief, often leaving a drier zone downwind. Convectional rainfall results from buoyant ascent, while frontal rainfall can form where contrasting air masses meet. Seasonal shifts in pressure and circulation help drive monsoon winds.

## Concepts in depth

Weather describes current or short-period atmospheric conditions. Climate summarises distributions over longer periods, including averages and extremes. Pressure gradients drive air motion, modified by rotation and friction. Humidity depends on temperature and actual moisture.

## How to apply it

Cooling moist air to its dew point can lead to condensation. Relative humidity can rise as air cools without adding water. One unusually rainy day does not determine a long-term change in rainfall frequency.

## Worked example

Two stations at similar latitude lie on opposite sides of a mountain range. Moist prevailing winds ascend toward the first station and descend toward the second. Expect heavier rainfall on the windward side and drier conditions in the rain shadow.

## Common mistake

A single unusually cool or hot day is a weather observation. A climate comparison requires a record long enough to assess distributions and trends.

## Practice

Why can an inland city have a greater seasonal temperature range than a coastal city at similar latitude?

**Answer:** Water heats and cools more slowly than land. Nearby ocean water moderates coastal temperatures.
