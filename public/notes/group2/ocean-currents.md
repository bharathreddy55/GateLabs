Surface ocean currents move water persistently under wind stress, Earth's rotation and the influence of basin boundaries. Large subtropical gyres generally rotate clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and counterclockwise in the Southern Hemisphere. Currents such as the Gulf Stream move warm water poleward. The California and Peru currents carry relatively cool water along eastern ocean-basin margins.

Differences in temperature and salinity affect water density and help drive overturning circulation. Winds can also cause surface water to diverge from a coast, drawing nutrient-rich water upward. This upwelling supports productivity and fisheries. In the northern Indian Ocean, seasonal monsoon winds influence current directions.

## Worked example

A question describes cold, nutrient-rich water surfacing near Peru. The process is upwelling. It supplies nutrients to the sunlit surface layer, supporting a productive food web.

## Common mistake

Keep a current's temperature relative to the surrounding waters in view. The labels warm and cold compare a current with the regional conditions it encounters.

## Practice

Why can seasonal wind reversal matter more in the northern Indian Ocean than in many subtropical gyres?

**Answer:** Monsoon winds reverse substantially between seasons, changing wind stress and the direction of some regional surface currents.
