The Gupta dynasty rose in northern India in the fourth century CE. Chandragupta I's marriage to Kumaradevi linked the dynasty with the Licchavis. Harishena's Prayaga Prashasti celebrates Samudragupta's military campaigns. Chandragupta II expanded into western India and defeated the Western Kshatrapas. Provincial and district officials, subordinate rulers and land grantees all appear in records.

Agriculture, commerce and craft production supported the economy. Gold coins show royal imagery and elite patronage. Land grants could transfer revenue claims and reveal ties between rulers, Brahmanas and religious institutions. Sanskrit court literature flourished, and Kalidasa is associated with the classical period. Hindu, Buddhist and Jain establishments coexisted. Deogarh's Dashavatara temple and Gupta-period sculptures from Sarnath show different traditions of religious art.

## Worked example

A question lists defeated northern rulers and southern rulers who were restored after submission. The pattern matches the Prayaga Prashasti's account of Samudragupta and illustrates different strategies of rule.

## Common mistake

The expression “golden age” usually describes selected cultural achievements. Use separate evidence for the living conditions of different groups.

## Quick revision

Harishena composed the Prayaga Prashasti. Chandragupta II defeated the Western Kshatrapas. Compare coins, land grants and inscriptions.

## Practice

What kind of source is the Prayaga Prashasti?

**Answer:** A Sanskrit royal eulogy. It offers evidence of political claims and campaigns and uses praise as part of its genre.
