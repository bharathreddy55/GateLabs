Ethnicity describes a sense of belonging formed around shared ancestry narratives, culture, language, history or territory. People hold overlapping identities, and the salience of each varies by context. Ethnic boundaries can change through migration, marriage, political organization and cultural revival. Analysts distinguish self-identification from classifications imposed by officials or researchers.

## Concepts in depth

Ethnicity may draw on perceived ancestry, heritage, language, place or memory. Identities are relational and can become more or less salient depending on institutions and events. Membership and identification can overlap with citizenship, class and religion.

## How to apply it

People can share a language but have distinct ethnic identities, while one ethnic community can live across several languages. A useful answer explains which boundary is relevant in a given case instead of assigning a fixed trait to every member.

## Worked example

A community celebrates a shared origin story and local tradition while its members follow different religions. A question about that common ancestry narrative concerns ethnicity.

## Common mistake

A shared language can be one marker of identity, but it cannot establish that everyone who speaks the language belongs to the same ethnic community.

## Practice

How can migration change ethnic organization?

**Answer:** Migrants may create associations and maintain selected customs in the destination, changing how identity is expressed.
