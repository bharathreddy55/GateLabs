The Cholas governed through a hierarchy often described as mandalam, valanadu, nadu and village. Revenue depended heavily on land and its assessment. Canals and tanks supported cultivation in the Kaveri delta. Under Rajaraja I and Rajendra I the kingdom expanded overseas and across parts of southern India. Temples received land and gifts and acted as centres of worship, employment and economic activity.

Inscriptions distinguish the ur of ordinary villages, the sabha of Brahmana settlements and the nagaram associated with merchants. Uttaramerur records set eligibility and disqualification rules for members of committees that handled local functions. The kudavolai process involved drawing names from a pot among eligible candidates. The records document particular institutions and settlements.

## Worked example

A question asks which body managed a Brahmadeya village through committees. Pick the sabha. If the clue concerns a merchant organization, look for nagaram.

## Common mistake

Avoid treating the Uttaramerur selection rules as universal adult voting. Participation was subject to qualifications specified in the inscriptions.

## Quick revision

Mandalam → province. Sabha → Brahmana village assembly. Kudavolai → pot selection from eligible candidates.

## Practice

What is the chief evidence for committee selection at Uttaramerur?

**Answer:** Inscriptions recording selection procedures, qualifications and grounds for disqualification.
