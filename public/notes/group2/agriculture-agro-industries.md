Indian cropping patterns reflect rainfall, irrigation, soils, labour, access to markets and policy. Kharif crops generally grow with the summer monsoon, while rabi crops occupy the cool season. Andhra Pradesh's irrigated Krishna–Godavari deltas support paddy. Dryland systems in parts of Rayalaseema include groundnut and millets. Horticulture, livestock and aquaculture add other sources of income, with aquaculture particularly important in suitable coastal tracts. Rice mills, oil mills and cold chains illustrate links between farms and processing.

## Concepts in depth

Crop distribution reflects rainfall, season, soils, irrigation, markets and policy. Agro-industries depend on farm products but may locate near producing areas, transport hubs or markets according to perishability, weight and processing needs.

## How to apply it

Sugar processing commonly favours proximity to cane because harvested cane is bulky and loses quality. Compare irrigated delta agriculture with rain-fed dryland cultivation without assuming one crop dominates all districts or seasons.

## Worked example

Given irrigated delta soils and a nearby rice mill, paddy links production and processing. In a rainfed interior area, crop choices depend more directly on rainfall timing and drought tolerance.

## Common mistake

A high yield and a high total output measure different things. Output depends on the area cultivated as well as yield.

## Practice

Why does a horticultural cluster benefit from cold storage?

**Answer:** It slows deterioration and gives producers more time to sort, transport and sell perishable produce.
