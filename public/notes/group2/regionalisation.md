Regionalisation refers to forming or identifying regions through economic, cultural, administrative or physical connections. Regionalism is political or social assertion grounded in regional identity or interests. Uneven development, language, geographic separation and representation can shape regional demands. The formation of Andhra State in 1953 illustrates language-based political mobilization.

## Concepts in depth

Regionalisation describes the formation or strengthening of regional ties and institutions. Regionalism refers to mobilisation around regional identity or demands. It may seek development, cultural recognition, autonomy or a new state, and is not inherently secessionist.

## How to apply it

A linguistic statehood movement should be analysed through identity, political representation and federal negotiation. A plea for improved resource allocation does not automatically call for administrative separation.

## Worked example

A planner defines a region using commuter flows. That is a functional regionalisation. An association demanding public investment for a neglected region illustrates regionalism.

## Common mistake

A region can be defined by a shared characteristic or by links around a centre. Read the criterion in the question.

## Practice

Why can uneven access to public services strengthen a regional demand?

**Answer:** Groups may interpret persistent gaps as underrepresentation and organize around a shared territorial interest.
