Tourism flows follow attractions, accessibility, seasonality and availability of services. India has pilgrimage, heritage, nature, coastal and business destinations. In Andhra Pradesh, Tirumala illustrates pilgrimage, Araku and the Borra Caves illustrate hill and geological tourism, and Amaravati provides a Buddhist heritage example. Tourism can create local work in hospitality, transport and guiding. Crowding, water demand and site degradation require visitor management.

## Concepts in depth

Tourism clusters around natural, cultural, religious and leisure assets supported by access and services. Seasonal demand creates variable pressures on infrastructure. Carrying capacity depends on physical limits, environmental effects and local community tolerance.

## How to apply it

A pilgrimage centre may need crowd and traffic management during peak periods, while a fragile coastal site may need habitat safeguards. Compare tourism receipts with distribution of benefits and costs to local residents.

## Worked example

If a sensitive cave site receives heavy weekend traffic, timed entry and guided routes can reduce crowding and protect fragile formations while sustaining visitation.

## Common mistake

A place can attract more than one kind of visitor. Categorize the chief reason for travel in the question.

## Practice

Why can seasonality be a problem for tourism employment?

**Answer:** Demand and revenue concentrate in peak periods, leaving workers and facilities underused at other times.
