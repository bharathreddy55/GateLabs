The East India Company gained political leverage through Bengal's succession disputes, defeated Siraj ud-Daulah at Plassey in 1757 and won at Buxar in 1764 against the combined forces of Mir Qasim, Shuja ud-Daula and Shah Alam II. The Diwani grant of 1765 gave it revenue rights in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa. Revenue financed an army and further expansion. Wars with Mysore, the Marathas and the Sikhs extended British control.

Under the Subsidiary Alliance, a ruler accepted British troops and restrictions on external relations, often paying for the force. Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse facilitated annexations where the Company refused to recognize an adopted heir for succession. Awadh was annexed in 1856 on the stated grounds of misgovernment.

## Worked example

Order four events: Buxar, Diwani, Plassey, and Awadh annexation. The timeline is Plassey 1757 → Buxar 1764 → Diwani 1765 → Awadh annexation 1856.

## Common mistake

Separate Awadh's stated annexation grounds from the Doctrine of Lapse used in places such as Jhansi.

## Practice

Which grant most directly expanded the Company's capacity to collect Bengal revenues?

**Answer:** The Diwani grant of 1765. It gave the Company revenue collection rights after the military success at Buxar.
