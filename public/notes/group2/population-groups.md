Study the syllabus's historical use of racial classifications in context. Human traits vary continuously and across populations, and fixed racial typologies lack a sound basis as biological divisions. In Indian administrative data, Scheduled Tribes are lists specified for particular states or territories. Adivasi communities have distinctive histories, languages and regional distributions. Andhra Pradesh's hill and forest tracts are home to several tribal communities, including Konda Dora and Savara communities in parts of the northern Eastern Ghats. Language use, mother tongue, religion and ethnicity measure distinct dimensions of identity.

## Concepts in depth

Population classifications use different bases including language, religion, statutory status and self-identification. These categories can overlap, and numeric comparisons need matching units and reference periods. So-called racial classifications are historical constructs and should not be treated as fixed biological hierarchies.

## How to apply it

A linguistic group can span several states and religions. Scheduled Tribe status is a constitutional classification applicable to specific notified communities in context. Never infer a person's full set of identities from just one reported classification.

## Worked example

To compare Telugu and Urdu mother-tongue counts, use Census table C-16 and check the geographic extent. For religious affiliation use table C-01. The two tables answer different questions.

## Common mistake

Avoid treating language, religion and tribal status as interchangeable categories. Comparisons across tables also need consistent boundaries.

## Practice

Why should a researcher state the Census reference year and area?

**Answer:** Population distribution and administrative boundaries can change. The reference year and area show exactly which population was counted.
