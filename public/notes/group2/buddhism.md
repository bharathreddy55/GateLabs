Buddhism developed amid urban growth, new kingdoms and competing renunciant traditions in the middle Ganga basin in the first millennium BCE. Siddhartha Gautama's teaching addresses dukkha, its origin in craving, its cessation and the path to cessation. The Eightfold Path includes right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration. The Sangha organized monastic life, while lay followers provided support and practised ethical conduct.

The Middle Way steers practice between sensual indulgence and severe self-mortification. Buddhist teaching emphasizes impermanence and examines how suffering arises through conditions. In later centuries, patronage and monastic networks encouraged Buddhist traditions to spread. Ashokan inscriptions are a source for Mauryan royal policy and should be read in their own historical context.

## Worked example

An item asks which Noble Truth identifies the origin of suffering. The second truth identifies craving as the origin. The fourth truth gives a practical response through the Eightfold Path.

## Common mistake

Keep the Buddha's teaching distinct from Ashoka's policy of dhamma. Ashokan edicts address broad public conduct, including restraint and respect for different religious groups.

## Quick revision

- Four Noble Truths → problem, origin, cessation, path.
- Middle Way → avoid the two extremes of indulgence and self-mortification.
- Sangha → community with an established monastic discipline.

## Practice

Which components of the Eightfold Path concern how one earns a living and how one speaks? **Answer:** Right livelihood and right speech, both part of ethical conduct.

What social institution helped transmit teachings across regions? **Answer:** The Sangha, supported by lay donors and sometimes rulers.
