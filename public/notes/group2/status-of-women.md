Assess women's status through education, paid work, income and asset control, health, unpaid care, representation and safety. Gains in one dimension may coexist with barriers in another. Gender intersects with class, caste, disability and location, so disaggregated evidence is important. Time-use surveys capture unpaid activities that ordinary employment figures can obscure.

## Concepts in depth

Analyse gender inequality through outcomes and underlying constraints, including access to education, paid work, property, time, safety and voice. Labour force participation includes active job-seeking under survey definitions. Unpaid care can constrain available time without appearing as paid employment.

## How to apply it

When comparing participation rates, match age groups, reference periods and measures. Higher enrolment does not automatically remove wage gaps or barriers to decision-making. Use disaggregated evidence to distinguish improvements for some groups from state-wide averages.

## Worked example

Two districts have similar female literacy, but women's paid work differs. Compare unpaid care, local jobs, transport and childcare before attributing the difference to attitudes alone.

## Common mistake

Labour force participation and employment are distinct. Participation includes jobseekers who meet the survey definition as well as employed people.

## Practice

Why does ownership matter alongside household income?

**Answer:** Control over land, savings or other assets affects a person's bargaining power, security and independent choices.
