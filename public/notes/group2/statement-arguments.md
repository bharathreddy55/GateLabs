Evaluate an argument by relevance, evidential support and connection to the proposed policy. Restate the question so an attractive but unrelated argument does not distract you. A strong argument may support or oppose the same proposal on different grounds.

## Worked example

Should the city install a crossing near a school? A documented high pedestrian injury rate at that location directly supports safety intervention. The popularity of the school gives little information about crossing risk.

## Common mistake

A vivid anecdote can illustrate a concern. It may carry less weight than evidence representative of the proposed location.

## Practice

Which is stronger for improving an unreliable bus route, verified delay records or the colour of the buses?

**Answer:** The delay records, because they address the reliability problem directly.
