Auxiliary be forms progressives and passives, have forms perfect constructions, and do supplies support for many simple present and past questions and negatives. Modals such as can, may, should and must express meanings including ability, possibility and obligation. In an object question, place the auxiliary before the subject. A subject question can retain ordinary order.

## Worked example

'She bought a book' becomes 'What did she buy?' The auxiliary did carries past tense, and buy returns to its base form. 'Who bought a book?' asks about the subject.

## Common mistake

Do not retain past tense on both did and the lexical verb. Use 'Did she go?', not 'Did she went?'

## Practice

Which modal usually expresses strong obligation in 'You ___ wear a seat belt'?

**Answer:** Must, when the intended meaning is a requirement.
