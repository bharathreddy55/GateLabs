Observation records behaviour in context. Anecdotal records capture specific incidents, rating scales apply defined criteria, and interviews explore learner perspectives. Reflective journals record patterns over time. A case study combines sources for one learner or setting. Longitudinal work follows change over time. Cross-cultural work compares settings. In classroom action research the teacher plans a change, acts, observes outcomes and reflects before revising the plan.

## Worked example

If several pupils miss instructions, the teacher compares participation before and after using visual instructions, while recording task type and alternatives that may explain improvement.

## Common mistake

Record what occurred before interpreting intention. 'Asked twice for the page number' is more informative than 'was lazy'.

## Practice

Why might a teacher interview a learner after observing repeated mistakes?

**Answer:** The interview can clarify how the learner reasoned and expose an explanation that was invisible from the answer alone.
