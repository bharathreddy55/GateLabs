Capitalize sentence beginnings and proper names. A full stop closes a statement, a question mark marks a direct question, and quotation marks identify exact speech or cited words. Use commas to separate list items or set off selected introductory and nonessential material. Apostrophes mark possession or omitted letters in contractions.

## Worked example

Write 'Ravi asked, “Are we leaving?”' with a capital at the beginning of the quotation and the question mark inside because the quoted words form a question.

## Common mistake

Its is possessive. It's contracts it is or it has. Ordinary plurals generally need no apostrophe.

## Practice

What punctuation helps address someone directly in 'Let's eat Grandma'?

**Answer:** A comma after 'eat': 'Let's eat, Grandma.' It separates the name used in direct address.
