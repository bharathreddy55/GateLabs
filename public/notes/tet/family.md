Family describes socially recognized relationships and responsibilities. A household groups people who usually live together and often pool resources. A household may contain unrelated people, and members of one family can live in several households. Nuclear households commonly contain parents and children, while extended and joint arrangements bring in other relatives. Migration and employment can divide residence while remittances and caregiving sustain joint obligations.

## Worked example

Two brothers live in separate cities and jointly support their parents. The residences are separate households, while kinship obligations link them.

## Common mistake

A household survey can measure residence and sharing arrangements more directly than emotional relationships.

## Practice

Why can an older parent move between adult children's homes?

**Answer:** Care obligations can be distributed across a family network even when its members occupy different households.
