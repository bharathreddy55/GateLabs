Moral reasoning develops as children interpret rules, consequences, intentions and fairness. Piaget contrasted externally imposed and cooperative understandings of rules. Kohlberg described patterns of reasoning from consequences and authority toward broader principles, with cultural context affecting interpretation. Play can be solitary, parallel, associative or cooperative and can involve physical activity, symbols and rules. Self-concept is one's description of oneself, while self-esteem concerns evaluation. Children develop self-control with practice and support.

## Worked example

Children disagree about whether an accidental rule break deserves punishment. Ask them to compare intention, consequences and fairness, then negotiate a shared rule.

## Common mistake

A rule-following answer can reflect several types of reasoning. Ask why the child thinks an action is right.

## Practice

Which kind of play involves coordinating roles toward a shared goal?

**Answer:** Cooperative play, because the players organize activity together.
