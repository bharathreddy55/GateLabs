Childhood is shaped by family, language, community and access to school, so children of the same age may have quite different experiences. Growth refers to measurable physical change, maturation to biologically influenced readiness, and development to changes in capacities and behaviour across domains. Physical and motor development includes gross movements such as running and fine control such as manipulating a pencil. Milestones are approximate guides. Health, nutrition, practice and supportive relationships affect how skills emerge. Over-age or returning learners benefit from assessing what they know and giving age-respectful support.

## Worked example

A child can explain a story but struggles to form letters. Offer oral response and fine-motor practice while continuing the same comprehension goal.

## Common mistake

A milestone observed later than average needs contextual assessment. One observation gives limited evidence.

## Practice

Which is primarily growth: gaining height or learning to cooperate?

**Answer:** Gaining height is physical growth. Learning to cooperate is social development.
