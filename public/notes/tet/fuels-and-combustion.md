Combustion commonly involves fuel reacting with oxygen and releasing energy. Ignition requires suitable conditions and sufficient temperature. Coal, petroleum and natural gas are exhaustible resources formed over geological time. Refining separates petroleum into useful fractions. Fuel value, efficiency, air pollution and greenhouse-gas emissions are distinct considerations. Incomplete combustion can produce carbon monoxide and soot.

## Worked example

A yellow luminous flame can contain hot soot particles that glow. Compare with a well-aerated blue flame when discussing combustion conditions.

## Common mistake

Efficiency describes useful energy out relative to energy in. It is different from fuel energy content per unit mass.

## Practice

Why does lack of oxygen affect a flame?

**Answer:** It can limit complete oxidation, altering flame behaviour and increasing incomplete combustion products.
