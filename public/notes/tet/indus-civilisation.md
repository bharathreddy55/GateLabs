The mature Harappan phase is usually dated to roughly 2600–1900 BCE. Sites across today's Pakistan and northwestern India formed a network of settlements with shared weights, seals and craft practices. Mohenjo-daro's Great Bath and drains show investment in public construction. Dholavira's reservoirs illustrate adaptation to water scarcity. Harappa, Kalibangan and Lothal are distinct sites, so match each find to its excavation context.

Farmers raised crops including wheat and barley. Bead makers worked materials such as carnelian, and seals and weights suggest organized exchange. Mesopotamian texts mention Meluhha, commonly associated with the Indus region. The script remains undeciphered. Explanations of urban decline include changing river systems, shifts in water availability and economic reorganization.

## Worked example

A question describes a settlement in Kutch with stone-built divisions and an extensive water storage system. Kutch narrows the region, and the reservoirs point to Dholavira. A large brick bathing structure would instead point to the Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro.

## Common mistake

Archaeological finds show what people built or used. A seal bearing an image cannot establish a named deity or political title without independent evidence.

## Quick revision

- Dholavira → reservoirs and settlement divisions.
- Mohenjo-daro → Great Bath and drains.
- Standardized weights → evidence of shared exchange practices.

## Practice

Which conclusion is best supported by standardized weights at multiple sites? **Answer:** Exchange and measurement followed shared conventions. Political control by a single ruler would require other evidence.

What limits historians' interpretation of seal inscriptions? **Answer:** The script is undeciphered, and most inscriptions are short.
