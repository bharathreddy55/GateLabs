Living systems use energy, grow, respond to stimuli, and have life cycles. Plants include herbs, shrubs and trees, while habitat groupings include aquatic and desert plants. Roots anchor and absorb, stems support and transport, leaves commonly perform photosynthesis, and flowers contribute to reproduction. Animals may lay eggs or give birth to live young. Beaks, body coverings, modes of movement and shelters reflect functions in habitats. Food-based groupings distinguish herbivores, carnivores and omnivores.

## Worked example

A duck's webbed feet provide a broad surface for pushing against water. Compare their function with a climbing bird's grasping feet.

## Common mistake

A plant that does not visibly move still shows characteristics of living things such as growth and response.

## Practice

Why are thick waxy surfaces useful to many desert plants?

**Answer:** They can reduce water loss from exposed plant surfaces.
