A main clause can stand independently. A subordinate clause functions within another clause, for example as a noun-like complement, adverbial or relative modifier. A finite clause contains a verb marked for tense or an appropriate finite auxiliary. A nonfinite clause may use an infinitive or participle. A first conditional often combines present tense in the if-clause with will in the result. A second conditional often uses a past form and would to present a hypothetical case.

## Worked example

In 'The book that she chose is lengthy', 'that she chose' is a relative clause modifying 'book'. In 'If it rains, we will stay inside', the if-clause states the condition.

## Common mistake

Check the role of the whole clause. A beginning such as 'that' can introduce a relative clause or another kind of subordinate clause.

## Practice

Identify the nonfinite clause in 'Walking home, I saw a heron'.

**Answer:** 'Walking home' is a participial nonfinite clause.
