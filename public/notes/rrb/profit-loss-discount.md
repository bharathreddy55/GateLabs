## What it means

Cost price (CP) is the seller’s cost, marked price (MP) is the list price, and selling price (SP) is the transaction amount. Profit and loss compare SP with CP. Discounts compare SP with MP. When costs include repairs or carriage charged to the seller, include them if the question asks for profit on total cost.

## Formulas and rules

- **Profit and loss:** Gain %=(SP−CP)/CP×100% if SP>CP. Loss %=(CP−SP)/CP×100% if SP<CP.
- **Discount:** SP=MP(1−d/100). A second discount applies to the already reduced price.
- **Target marking:** MP=CP(1+p/100)/(1−d/100) to earn p% after a discount d% below 100.
- **Combined sale:** Add the individual cost and selling prices, then calculate the overall gain or loss as a fraction of total cost.

## How to study it for Station Master

- Sketch CP → MP → SP and write percentage multipliers over the arrows.
- Mix questions with equal costs and equal selling prices. Compute the total cost before expecting gains and losses to cancel.

## Question models and worked examples

### Markup and discount

**Question.** An item costs ₹960, is marked up 35% and is discounted 20%. Find profit percent.

1. MP=960×1.35=₹1296. SP=1296×0.80=₹1036.80.
2. Profit ₹76.80 is 76.80/960=0.08 of CP.

**Answer:** 8% profit.

### Marking for a target profit

**Question.** Cost is ₹850. What marked price gives a 20% profit after a 15% discount?

1. Target SP=850×1.20=₹1020.
2. The buyer pays 85% of MP, so MP=1020/0.85.

**Answer:** ₹1200.

### Equal selling prices

**Question.** Two goods sell for ₹600 each, one at 20% gain and one at 20% loss. Find the overall result.

1. Cost prices are 600/1.20=₹500 and 600/0.80=₹750.
2. Total receipts are ₹1200 against ₹1250 spent, a loss of ₹50.

**Answer:** 4% loss.

### Successive versus single discount

**Question.** Which price is lower: 20% and then 10% off, or a single 29% off?

1. Successive discounts retain 0.8×0.9=0.72 of MP.
2. One discount retains 0.71. Compare amounts paid on the same marked price.

**Answer:** The single 29% discount is cheaper by 1% of MP.
