## Basics first

- **Cost price.** CP is what the seller pays, including any expenses explicitly counted as costs. The gain or loss rate uses CP as its base.
- **Marked and selling prices.** MP is the listed price. SP is the amount collected after discount. The discount rate uses MP or the current discounted price as its base.
- **Profit or loss.** Profit is SP − CP. A negative result is a loss. Comparing combined costs and combined receipts gives the overall percentage.
- **Markup and margin.** Markup is profit or price increase relative to cost. If a prompt asks for profit margin, read its definition carefully. Margin commonly uses selling price as its denominator.

## Methods and when to use them

### Method A · Follow the price chain

Write CP → MP → SP. Apply a markup to CP, then multiply MP by the fraction remaining after discount. Finally compute the profit rate using the original CP.

### Method B · Work backwards from a target

Start by calculating the SP that provides the required profit or loss. If the discount is known, divide target SP by the fraction retained after discount to find MP.

### Method C · Combine by totals

When different items have equal SPs, recover each CP by division. Add receipts and costs separately. A gain and a loss of the same percent generally act on different costs.

## Question models and worked examples

### Markup and discount

**Question.** An item costs ₹960, is marked up 35% and is discounted 20%. Find profit percent.

1. **Mark up from the cost.** The marked price is 135% of ₹960, so it is 960 × 1.35 = ₹1,296.\
   `960 × 1.35 = ₹1,296`
2. **Discount the marked price.** The buyer pays 80% of ₹1,296. The selling price is ₹1,036.80.\
   `1,296 × 0.80 = ₹1,036.80`
3. **Measure profit against cost.** Profit is ₹1,036.80 − ₹960 = ₹76.80. Divide by ₹960 and multiply by 100% to get 8%.\
   `(1,036.80 − 960) ÷ 960 × 100% = 8%`

**Answer:** 8% profit.

### Marking for a target profit

**Question.** Cost is ₹850. What marked price gives a 20% profit after a 15% discount?

1. **Find the required selling price.** A 20% gain means the seller needs 120% of the ₹850 cost, or 850 × 1.20 = ₹1,020.\
   `850 × 1.20 = ₹1,020`
2. **Relate the sale to the marked price.** A 15% discount leaves 85% of the marked price. If M is the marked price, 0.85M = 1,020.\
   `0.85 × MP = 1,020`
3. **Solve and confirm.** M = 1,020 ÷ 0.85 = ₹1,200. Taking ₹180 off this price leaves the target ₹1,020.\
   `1,020 ÷ 0.85 = ₹1,200`

**Answer:** ₹1200.

### Equal selling prices

**Question.** Two goods sell for ₹600 each, one at 20% gain and one at 20% loss. Find the overall result.

1. **Recover each cost from its own multiplier.** The profit item sells for 1.2 times its cost, so its cost was 600 ÷ 1.2 = ₹500. The loss item sells at 0.8 times its cost, so its cost was ₹750.\
   `600 ÷ 1.20 = ₹500`
2. **Add receipts and costs.** The seller receives ₹1,200 against costs of ₹500 + ₹750 = ₹1,250. That makes a ₹50 loss.\
   `600 ÷ 0.80 = ₹750`
3. **Use the combined cost as the base.** Overall loss percentage is 50 ÷ 1,250 × 100% = 4%. Both equal selling prices conceal different cost bases.\
   `(1,200 − 1,250) ÷ 1,250 × 100% = −4%`

**Answer:** 4% loss.

### Successive versus single discount

**Question.** Which price is lower: 20% and then 10% off, or a single 29% off?

1. **Find the retained fraction for two discounts.** A 20% discount leaves 0.8 of the marked price. The next discount leaves 0.9 of that reduced amount, so the buyer pays 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72.\
   `0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72`
2. **Find the retained fraction for one discount.** A 29% discount leaves 0.71 of the same marked price.\
   `1 − 0.29 = 0.71`
3. **Compare amounts paid.** Since 0.71 is smaller than 0.72, the single discount wins. The difference is 0.01 of the marked price.\
   `0.72 − 0.71 = 0.01 of MP`

**Answer:** The single 29% discount is cheaper by 1% of MP.

## Quick revision sheet

| When you see | Use this rule |
| --- | --- |
| Marked up by m% | MP = CP × (1 + m/100). |
| Discount d% | SP = MP × (1 − d/100). |
| Profit percent | (SP − CP) ÷ CP × 100%. |
| Two discounts d₁ and d₂ | Retained fraction = (1 − d₁/100)(1 − d₂/100). |

## Exam tips

- **Draw arrows between prices.** Annotate each arrow with its own multiplier.
- **An equal rupee result uses a different base.** A ₹600 sale at a profit has lower cost than a ₹600 sale at a loss.
- **Compare final fractions.** The lower retained proportion gives the better discount from the same marked price.
