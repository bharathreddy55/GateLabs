## Basics first

- **Whole circle.** A full pie is 360° and represents the whole population. Each sector’s fraction of 360° equals its share of that pie.
- **Sector labels.** Labels may give degrees, percentages, fractions or counts. Use a common representation before adding or comparing.
- **Unlabelled share.** If all sectors cover the whole, subtract labelled shares from 100% or labelled angles from 360°.
- **Pies of different sizes.** The angle compares parts as a fraction of their own totals. A smaller angle in a much larger group can represent more items.

## Methods and when to use them

### Method A · Convert through a fraction of the whole

For angle θ, write θ/360. Multiply by the overall count to get the sector count. To find angle from a count, take count/whole and multiply by 360°.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Pie Charts / Sector Counts and Angles** questions when the total is known and you need to convert between a sector’s angle, percentage or count.

### Method B · Recover a missing total

Calculate the known sector’s fraction p. Its count is p times the total, so divide the sector count by p. Check by multiplying the new total by p again.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Pie Charts / Recovering the Whole** questions when a sector’s count and angle or percentage are given but the total is missing.

### Method C · Align denominators on comparisons

Convert every sector to its own count if asked “how many more.” If only proportions are requested, degrees can be compared directly because both use 360°.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Pie Charts / Comparing Different Totals** questions when two charts describe different groups or years and the prompt asks for a difference in counts or shares.

## Question models and worked examples

### Convert an angle to count

**Question.** A 72° sector represents emergency calls out of 1,850 total calls. How many are emergencies?

**Picture it.** Imagine 1,850 calls sharing the whole circle. A slice taking one fifth of the circle gets one fifth of the calls.

1. **Convert the angle into a share.** The whole circle spans 360°. Emergency calls take 72/360 = 1/5 of it.\
   `72/360 = 1/5`
2. **Match the share to the count.** The one-fifth share applies to all 1,850 calls, so multiply 1,850 by 1/5.\
   `1,850 × 1/5 = 370`
3. **Check the conversion.** The result is 370 calls. Five sectors of that size would account for the full 1,850.\
   `370 × 5 = 1,850`

**Answer:** 370.

### Recover the whole

**Question.** A 54° maintenance sector represents 150 tasks. Find the total.

**Picture it.** The small maintenance slice holds 150 tasks. Count how many slices that size would fit into the whole before recovering the total.

1. **Find what proportion 150 represents.** A 54° sector is 54/360 = 0.15 of the full pie.\
   `54/360 = 0.15`
2. **Set up the whole-count equation.** If T is the total, then 0.15T = 150 tasks.\
   `0.15T = 150`
3. **Recover and check T.** Divide by 0.15 to get 1,000. Taking 15% of 1,000 gives the stated 150 tasks.\
   `T = 150/0.15 = 1,000`

**Answer:** 1000 tasks.

### Compare charts with unequal totals

**Question.** A’s 90° sector of 800 tasks and B’s 72° sector of 1250 tasks represent inspections. Compare the counts.

**Picture it.** The angles show how the tasks are split in each circle. The two circles stand for different numbers of tasks, so convert the slices to real counts first.

1. **Convert A’s sector to a count.** 90° is a quarter of the pie, so A has 800 × 1/4 = 200 inspections.\
   `800 × 90/360 = 200`
2. **Convert B’s sector using B’s own total.** 72° is one-fifth of the pie, so B has 1,250 × 1/5 = 250 inspections.\
   `1,250 × 72/360 = 250`
3. **Compare counts.** B has 250 − 200 = 50 more. A’s sector takes a greater share of a smaller whole.\
   `250 − 200 = 50`

**Answer:** B has 50 more.

### Find the unlabelled sector

**Question.** A pie chart’s labelled sectors are 35%, 30% and 72°. Find the remaining sector angle.

**Picture it.** Some pieces are marked as percentages and one in degrees. Give them all the same kind of label before checking how much circle is left.

1. **Use one unit for all sectors.** Convert the 72° sector to 72/360 × 100% = 20%. The other labels already use percent.\
   `72/360 = 20%`
2. **Find the unlabelled share.** The three displayed shares add to 35% + 30% + 20% = 85%, so 15% remains.\
   `100% − 35% − 30% − 20% = 15%`
3. **Convert that share back to degrees.** 15% of 360° is 0.15 × 360° = 54°.\
   `0.15 × 360° = 54°`

**Answer:** 54°.

## Exam tips

- **Use the correct pie.** A’s percentages cannot be multiplied by B’s total.
- **Keep exact fractions.** Reduce 72/360 to 1/5 and 90/360 to 1/4 for quick calculation.
- **Check for rounded shares.** If a chart says percentages are rounded, derived counts may be approximate.
