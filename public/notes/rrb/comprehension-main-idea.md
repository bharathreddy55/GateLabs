## What it means

A main idea is the central point that connects a passage’s important parts. The topic identifies what the passage is about, while the main idea says what the author wants you to understand about it. Supporting ideas provide reasons, examples, qualifications or evidence. A good title covers the passage’s scope without merely naming one striking detail.

## Formulas and rules

- **Main-idea rule:** Form a short topic-plus-claim sentence and check whether it explains why each main paragraph is present.
- **Detail rule:** For a factual question, find the supporting line and check any time, quantity or exception in its wording.
- **Scope check:** Reject answers that are narrower than most of the passage or broader than its evidence.
- **Paraphrase check:** Keep the original strength. “May”, “often” and “under these conditions” should survive a paraphrase.

## How to study it for Station Master

- Read an English passage once for its overall direction and mark each paragraph in two or three words. Answer line-based details by returning to the relevant sentence.
- For review, write why your strongest distractor was tempting, such as a memorable example or a repeated word. Check it against the passage as a whole.

## Question models and worked examples

### Main point across contrasting details

**Question.** A park installed brighter lights to help visitors after sunset. Insects clustered near the new lamps, and walkers reported glare. The council switched to shielded lighting along paths and left the wetland dark. What is the main idea?

1. The first two sentences establish both the goal and the unintended problems.
2. The last sentence shows an adjustment that addresses both. A summary solely about insects misses the lighting goal.

**Answer:** The park adjusted its lighting to improve visibility while addressing glare and ecological effects.

### Supporting detail with a qualifier

**Question.** A museum opens at 9 am on weekdays. On Wednesdays the public galleries open at 11 to allow school groups early access. The cafe still opens at 9. When can a member of the public enter the galleries on Wednesday?

1. Locate the Wednesday exception and identify its subject, the public galleries.
2. The 9 am exception in the last line applies to the cafe only.

**Answer:** At 11 am.

### Best title

**Question.** A study compared three ways of restoring a stream. Concrete barriers redirected water briefly. Rooted native plants held soil through the monsoon, and their benefits grew as they established. Choose a title: “Monsoon Rainfall”, “Using Native Plants to Stabilise Stream Banks”, or “How Concrete Is Made”.

1. The passage uses the monsoon to test the methods, so rainfall alone is too narrow and does not express the result.
2. The strongest title names the subject and the finding about native vegetation.

**Answer:** “Using Native Plants to Stabilise Stream Banks”.

### Preserve a qualification in a summary

**Question.** Bread baked in an insulated oven cooled more slowly than bread from an uninsulated one. The difference was detectable indoors, but outdoors strong wind reduced it. Summarise the finding.

1. Keep the comparative result and specify the condition that weakened it.
2. Reject “insulation always keeps bread hot”. The passage measures relative cooling under two conditions.

**Answer:** Insulation slowed cooling in the trial, and strong outdoor wind reduced the difference.
