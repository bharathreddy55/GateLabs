## Basics first

- **Mass and weight.** Mass is measured in kilograms and stays the same when an object moves to another gravitational field. Weight is the gravitational force mg, in newtons.
- **Density.** Density is mass divided by volume. One gram per cubic centimetre equals 1000 kg/m³. On these scales ordinary water is approximately 1 g/cm³.
- **Displacement.** A fully submerged object pushes aside a volume of liquid equal to its external volume. The rise in a graduated cylinder gives that displaced volume.
- **Buoyancy.** The upward force is the weight of displaced fluid. A freely floating object displaces just enough fluid to balance its own weight.

## Methods and when to use them

### Method A · Separate mass from weight

Read whether a value is in kg or N before applying a formula. Weight changes in proportion to g. Solve m = W/g when the question gives a weight and field strength.

### Method B · Use the total external volume

For a sealed hollow body, divide its total mass by its outside volume. An air-filled space raises the external volume without adding much mass, which can lower the average density below the fluid’s.

### Method C · Balance buoyancy for flotation

Set ρfluid × g × Vsub equal to ρobject × g × Vtotal. Cancelling g gives a ratio of submerged to total volume. Only apply the floating-fraction formula to a floating object in equilibrium.

## Question models and worked examples

### Two gravitational fields

**Question.** What does a 12 kg tool weigh where g=10 m/s² and where g=1.6 m/s²?

**Picture it.** Imagine taking the same toolbox to a place where gravity feels weaker. You bring the same amount of stuff with you, but the pull on that stuff changes.

1. **Separate mass from weight.** The tool remains 12 kg at both locations. Its weight is the local gravitational force, given by W = mg.\
   `m = 12 kg in both places`
2. **Use the first field strength.** At g = 10 m/s², W = 12 × 10 = 120 N.\
   `W₁ = 12 × 10 = 120 N`
3. **Use the second field strength.** At 1.6 m/s², W = 12 × 1.6 = 19.2 N. The lower gravity reduces the weight without changing the mass.\
   `W₂ = 12 × 1.6 = 19.2 N`

**Answer:** 120 N and 19.2 N.

### Density by displacement

**Question.** A fully submerged 156 g stone raises water in a measuring cylinder from 90 to 150 mL. Find its density.

**Picture it.** When the stone goes underwater, it takes up room. The water rises to make space. The increase in the reading tells you how much space the stone took.

1. **Read the displaced amount.** Subtract the initial cylinder reading from the final reading. The stone has displaced 150 − 90 = 60 mL.\
   `150 − 90 = 60 mL`
2. **Match the volume units.** The stone is fully submerged, so its volume equals that displaced amount. Since 1 mL = 1 cm³, its volume is 60 cm³.\
   `60 mL = 60 cm³`
3. **Calculate density.** Average density is mass ÷ volume. Divide 156 g by 60 cm³ to get 2.6 g/cm³.\
   `156 ÷ 60 = 2.6 g/cm³`

**Answer:** 2.6 g/cm³.

### Average density with a hollow centre

**Question.** A sealed object of mass 360 g displaces 500 cm³ when fully immersed. Find its average density and whether it floats in water if released.

**Picture it.** Imagine two sealed lunch boxes of the same size, one filled and one partly empty. Compare the mass for the whole outside size, then compare that number with water.

1. **Use the object’s external volume.** Full immersion displaces 500 cm³. Include the hollow region when using the volume of the sealed object as a whole.\
   `external volume = 500 cm³`
2. **Find the average density.** Divide total mass by the displaced volume, 360 ÷ 500 = 0.72 g/cm³.\
   `360 ÷ 500 = 0.72 g/cm³`
3. **Compare with water.** Water is about 1 g/cm³. The object reaches a weight balance while only part of its volume is submerged, so it floats.\
   `0.72 < 1 g/cm³`

**Answer:** 0.72 g/cm³, and it floats.

### Floating fraction

**Question.** A wooden block has density 650 kg/m³ and floats in water with density 1000 kg/m³. What fraction is submerged?

**Picture it.** Picture a floating block held up by the water it pushes away. A lighter block only has to push away enough water to match its own weight.

1. **Set up the floating balance.** The object’s weight equals the buoyant force. The weight of displaced water therefore equals the weight of the block.\
   `ρwater g Vsub = ρblock g Vtotal`
2. **Cancel common factors.** Writing ρwater × Vsubmerged × g = ρblock × Vtotal × g gives Vsubmerged/Vtotal = ρblock/ρwater.\
   `Vsub/Vtotal = ρblock/ρwater`
3. **Substitute the densities.** 650 ÷ 1,000 = 0.65, so 65% of the block’s volume is submerged.\
   `650/1000 = 0.65 = 65%`

**Answer:** 65% of the volume.

## Exam tips

- **Compare densities in the same units.** Convert g/cm³ before comparing with a kg/m³ value.
- **Separate full immersion and flotation.** Full immersion displaces the entire outer volume. Floating generally displaces only part.
- **Check physical range.** An estimated floating fraction above 1 suggests the object cannot float on its own in that fluid.
