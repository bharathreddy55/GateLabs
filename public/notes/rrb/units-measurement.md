Use coherent units before applying a formula. SI base units relevant here include metre, kilogram, second, ampere and kelvin. Derived units combine these, such as newton = kg·m/s². Scientific notation and prefixes help separate scale from unit dimensions.

## Concepts in depth

Write units alongside values. As a length unit changes, an area conversion squares the factor and a volume conversion cubes it. Prefixes such as milli and kilo apply to the base quantity. Distinguish precision from accuracy when interpreting readings.

## How to apply it

1 cm³=1,000 mm³ and 1 L=1,000 cm³. A plate 24 cm by 35 cm by 2 mm occupies 168 cm³. Power in watts is J/s, so multiplying by seconds gives stored or transferred energy in joules.

## Worked example

36 km/h = 36×1000/3600 m/s = 10 m/s. A kilometre squared equals one million square metres.

## Common mistake

A length conversion factor must be squared for area and cubed for volume.

## Practice

Convert 250 cm³ to m³.

**Answer:** 0.00025 m³, using 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³.
