## Basics first

- **Measurement.** A measurement pairs a number with a unit. The same amount can be written as 1 m or 100 cm, with the magnitude unchanged.
- **Base and derived units.** Mass uses kg, length m and duration s. Derived units combine base units. A newton is kg·m/s² and a joule is a newton metre.
- **Prefixes and powers.** Centimetre means 10⁻² m. Squaring or cubing a length unit also squares or cubes its conversion factor.
- **Zero error.** The reading on a closed instrument shows a signed offset. Corrected reading is display minus that offset. A negative zero error therefore increases the corrected reading.

## Methods and when to use them

### Method A · Cancel units along the calculation

Multiply by a ratio equal to one, choosing the orientation that cancels the old unit. For density convert mass and volume separately before dividing. Treat cm³ as three factors of cm.

### Method B · Expand the derived unit

Start from a physical equation. For mechanical work, use force times displacement. Express force as mass times acceleration, then multiply by metres to get kg·m²/s².

### Method C · Separate resolution from offset

Resolution is the smallest change shown. Zero error is a systematic shift that can occur even with fine resolution. Correct the offset before rounding to instrument precision.

## Question models and worked examples

### Convert volume units

**Question.** Convert 0.036 m³ to litres and cm³.

1. **Convert cubic metres to litres.** 1 m³ = 1,000 L, so 0.036 m³ equals 0.036 × 1,000 = 36 L.\
   `0.036 × 1,000 = 36 L`
2. **Convert litres to cubic centimetres.** 1 L = 1,000 cm³. Therefore 36 L equals 36 × 1,000 = 36,000 cm³.\
   `36 × 1,000 = 36,000 cm³`
3. **Check with the cube of the length conversion.** Since 1 m = 100 cm, 1 m³ = 100³ cm³ = 1,000,000 cm³. Multiplying by 0.036 gives the same result.\
   `0.036 × 100³ = 36,000 cm³`

**Answer:** 36 L or 36,000 cm³.

### Convert a compound unit

**Question.** Convert 2.7 g/cm³ to kg/m³.

1. **Convert the numerator.** 2.7 g = 2.7 ÷ 1,000 = 0.0027 kg.\
   `2.7 g = 0.0027 kg`
2. **Cube the denominator’s conversion.** Since 1 cm = 0.01 m, one cubic centimetre is (0.01)³ m³ = 0.000001 m³.\
   `1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³`
3. **Divide in the new units.** 0.0027 kg ÷ 0.000001 m³ = 2,700 kg/m³. The small converted denominator makes the numeric value larger.\
   `0.0027 ÷ 10⁻⁶ = 2,700 kg/m³`

**Answer:** 2700 kg/m³.

### Identify derived dimensions

**Question.** Express one joule in SI base units.

1. **Start from mechanical work.** Work = force × displacement along the force. One joule therefore equals 1 N·m.\
   `1 J = 1 N·m`
2. **Express the force in base units.** From F = ma, a newton is a kilogram times metres per second squared, or kg·m/s².\
   `1 N = 1 kg·m/s²`
3. **Include the displacement unit.** Multiply kg·m/s² by one additional metre, giving kg·m²/s².\
   `1 J = 1 kg·m²/s²`

**Answer:** kg·m²/s².

### Correct zero error

**Question.** A caliper reads +0.03 cm when closed and 1.82 cm on a rod. What is the corrected diameter?

1. **Read the zero offset.** With the jaws closed the true reading is zero. A displayed +0.03 cm means readings are too high by 0.03 cm.\
   `zero reading = +0.03 cm`
2. **Apply the signed correction.** Corrected value = indicated value − zero reading. Here subtract the positive offset.\
   `corrected = display − zero reading`
3. **Calculate the diameter.** 1.82 cm − 0.03 cm = 1.79 cm, slightly below the displayed reading.\
   `1.82 − 0.03 = 1.79 cm`

**Answer:** 1.79 cm.

## Exam tips

- **Carry written units through the steps.** They reveal a numerator or denominator flipped by mistake.
- **Put an exponent on the conversion too.** 1 cm³ = (10⁻² m)³.
- **Keep the offset’s sign.** Subtract the signed value shown when the instrument should read zero.
