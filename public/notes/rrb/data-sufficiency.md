Decide if the information produces a unique answer to the actual question. Test statements individually, then together if needed. To establish insufficiency, exhibit two cases satisfying the information with different answers.

## Concepts in depth

A statement is sufficient when it uniquely determines the requested value or decision. Test each statement separately using the stem. If neither suffices, combine them. A uniquely determined yes/no answer can suffice even when exact quantities remain unknown.

## How to apply it

If X has three times Y's customers and takes twice as long, its rate is 1.5 times Y's. Each fact alone leaves the rate comparison open. Do not assume that a statement is necessary simply because it supplies a number.

## Worked example

What is x? Statement I gives x+y=10, and II gives y=4. Neither alone fixes x. Together they give x=6.

## Common mistake

A yes-or-no question is sufficient when the answer is always yes or always no under the given conditions.

## Practice

Is integer n even? Statement: n is a multiple of 6.

**Answer:** Sufficient. Every integer multiple of 6 is even.
