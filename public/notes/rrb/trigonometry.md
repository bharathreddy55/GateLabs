## Basics first

- **Right-triangle ratios.** For a chosen acute angle, sine is opposite/hypotenuse, cosine is adjacent/hypotenuse and tangent is opposite/adjacent.
- **Reference angle.** The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. Changing the acute reference angle swaps which leg is opposite and which is adjacent.
- **Elevation and depression.** Both angles are measured from a horizontal. In a height problem, the tangent relates vertical rise to horizontal ground distance.
- **Exact values.** sin 30° = 1/2, cos 30° = √3/2, and tan 30° = 1/√3. Both sin 45° and cos 45° equal 1/√2 and tan 45° = 1. Complementary 30° and 60° swap sine and cosine.

## Methods and when to use them

### Method A · Recover a missing leg

Treat a reduced sine or cosine as a scaled pair of side lengths. Use Pythagoras to fill in the third side. Then form the requested ratio using the same reference angle.

### Method B · Work from a horizontal through the observer

Use the horizontal distance as the adjacent leg and the rise above the eye as the opposite leg. Add the observer’s eye height to get the total object height when both stand on the same level.

### Method C · Equate two expressions for one height

When nearer and farther observations are on the same line from the base, call their distances x and x plus the known gap. Both tangent expressions give the same vertical height. Solve the distance equation first.

## Question models and worked examples

### Find remaining ratios

**Question.** Given sin θ=5/13 for acute θ, find cos θ and tan θ.

1. **Build a right triangle.** Sine is opposite/hypotenuse. Take those side lengths as 5 and 13, which preserve the ratio 5/13.\
   `opposite = 5 and hypotenuse = 13`
2. **Find the remaining leg.** Using Pythagoras gives adjacent² = 13² − 5² = 144. Take the positive length 12.\
   `adjacent = √(13² − 5²) = 12`
3. **Use the same reference angle.** Cosine is adjacent/hypotenuse = 12/13. Tangent is opposite/adjacent = 5/12.\
   `cos θ = 12/13 and tan θ = 5/12`

**Answer:** 12/13 and 5/12.

### Observer height

**Question.** A person’s eye level is 1.6 m above the ground. From 18 m away the pole top has elevation 45°. Find the pole height.

1. **Draw the line at eye level.** The angle of elevation is measured from the observer’s horizontal sight line. The 18 m ground distance is also the adjacent horizontal distance.\
   `horizontal distance = 18 m, eye height = 1.6 m`
2. **Find the rise above the eye.** Tangent = rise ÷ adjacent, so the rise is 18 × tan 45° = 18 m.\
   `rise = 18 × tan 45° = 18 m`
3. **Add the eye height.** The pole starts at ground level, 1.6 m below the eye line. Its full height is 18 + 1.6 = 19.6 m.\
   `18 + 1.6 = 19.6 m`

**Answer:** 19.6 m.

### Two stations of observation

**Question.** Two observation points at ground level lie on the same straight line from the base of a tower and on the same side of it. The points are 20 m apart, and their angles of elevation to the top are 60° and 30°. Find the height.

1. **Name the two distances.** Let the nearer point be x metres from the base. Its angle is 60°, while the farther point at x + 20 metres sees 30°.\
   `near = x, far = x + 20`
2. **Write the same height twice.** Using tangent, h = x tan 60° = x√3. The farther point gives h = (x + 20) tan 30° = (x + 20)/√3.\
   `h = √3 x = (x + 20)/√3`
3. **Solve and substitute.** Equating heights gives 3x = x + 20, so x = 10 m. Substitute this into h = x√3.\
   `3x = x + 20 ⇒ x = 10`\
   `h = 10√3 m`

**Answer:** 10√3 m.

### Exact value and identity

**Question.** Evaluate 1−sin²30°+tan²45°.

1. **Recall the exact ratios.** sin 30° = 1/2 and tan 45° = 1.\
   `sin 30° = 1/2, tan 45° = 1`
2. **Square the ratios first.** sin²30° = (1/2)² = 1/4, while tan²45° = 1² = 1.\
   `sin² 30° = 1/4, tan² 45° = 1`
3. **Combine the terms.** Now evaluate 1 − 1/4 + 1 = 2 − 1/4 = 7/4.\
   `1 − 1/4 + 1 = 7/4`

**Answer:** 7/4.

## Quick revision sheet

| When you see | Use this rule |
| --- | --- |
| Known opposite and adjacent | tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent. |
| Missing third side | Use a² + b² = c² with c as hypotenuse. |
| Height from eye level | Height = horizontal distance × tan θ + eye height on level ground. |
| Identity | sin² θ + cos² θ = 1. Also tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ where cosine is nonzero. |

## Exam tips

- **Draw the angle at the observer.** The question’s rise is opposite the horizontal viewing angle.
- **Check which point is nearer.** On the same level, the larger elevation to a fixed top means the shorter horizontal distance.
- **Keep radicals exact.** Compare options before converting √3 to a rounded decimal.
