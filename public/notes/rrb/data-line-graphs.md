Line graphs commonly show ordered observations over time. Distinguish the level at a point, absolute change, percentage change and slope. If time intervals differ, divide by elapsed time before comparing rates.

## Concepts in depth

A line chart shows ordered observations, often over time. Highest level, biggest absolute rise and steepest rate per unit time can be different. Check time spacing and units. Connecting points does not establish exact values between observations.

## How to apply it

If a value moves from 40 to 70 in six months, its average monthly increase is 5. A rise of 20 in two months is steeper at 10 per month, despite the smaller total change.

## Worked example

A value rises from 20 to 50 over three years, then from 50 to 70 in one year. The changes are 30 and 20, but the rates are 10 and 20 units per year.

## Common mistake

The line between recorded points often interpolates. It does not establish exact intermediate observations.

## Practice

What does a flat segment indicate on a chart of cumulative sales?

**Answer:** No additional net sales recorded during that interval, because the cumulative total is constant.
