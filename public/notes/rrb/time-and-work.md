For a fixed job, rate equals work divided by time. Set the job to one unit, add simultaneous independent work rates and subtract rates for leaks or undoing work. Break the timeline into intervals when someone joins or leaves.

## Concepts in depth

A worker completing one job in t days contributes 1/t job per day at constant rate. Add productive rates and subtract leaks or undoing work. When staffing changes, calculate work done in each phase before solving the remainder.

## How to apply it

A takes 12 days and B takes 18. In four days together they complete 4(1/12+1/18)=5/9. If A leaves, B needs (4/9)×18=8 days more. Avoid adding completion times directly.

## Worked example

A finishes in 6 days and B in 12 days. Combined rate is 1/6 + 1/12 = 1/4 of the job per day. They need four days.

## Common mistake

Add rates rather than completion times. Confirm that the individual rates refer to the same complete job.

## Practice

A pipe fills a tank in 4 hours. An outlet drains it in 12 hours. How quickly does it fill with both open?

**Answer:** 6 hours, because net fill rate is 1/4 − 1/12 = 1/6 tank per hour.
