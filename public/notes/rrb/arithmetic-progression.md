An arithmetic progression has a constant difference d. Its nth term is a+(n−1)d and the sum of its first n terms is n(a+last)/2. The difference may be negative or zero.

## Concepts in depth

An AP has a fixed difference d. The kth term is a+(k−1)d. Its first n terms sum to n(first+last)/2 because each mirrored pair has the same sum. To sum a range of terms, compute its endpoints and inclusive count.

## How to apply it

Terms 4 through 10 have seven terms, not six. If their endpoints are 23 and 53, the sum is 7×38=266. The common difference is (53−23)/(10−4)=5. For an odd-length run the middle term is the average. With an even length use the mean of the two middle terms.

## Worked example

For 5, 8, 11, …, term 12 is 5+11×3=38. The sum of the first 12 terms is 12(5+38)/2=258.

## Common mistake

There are n−1 intervals between the first and nth terms.

## Practice
