## Basics first

- **Perimeter, area and volume.** Perimeter measures boundary length, area measures covering and volume measures filled space. Their units have powers 1, 2 and 3 respectively.
- **Areas of common bases.** Rectangle area = length × width, triangle area = base × perpendicular height ÷ 2 and circle area = πr². The radius is half the diameter.
- **Sectors and rings.** A θ° sector uses θ/360 of a full circle. An annular sector subtracts the inner sector from the outer one at the same angle.
- **Curved walls and capacity.** A cylindrical wall unwraps to circumference × height. For a cylinder its capacity is base area × perpendicular height, and for a cone the factor is one third.

## Methods and when to use them

### Method A · Subtract two complete areas

For a constant border x around a rectangle, add 2x to each outside dimension, find the outer area and subtract the inner one. Check corner squares are included once.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Mensuration / Borders and Shaded Areas** questions when asked for the area of a path, frame or ring between an inner and an outer boundary.

**Key rule.** Border area = outer area − inner area. An annular sector uses the angle fraction θ ÷ 360° of that difference.

#### Type 1 · Path around a rectangle

**Question.** A 20 m by 14 m garden has a 1 m wide path outside all four edges. Find path area.

**Picture it.** Draw the garden with a frame around it. Both its left and right edges gain a metre, and so do its top and bottom edges. Take away the inside rectangle.

1. **Find the outer rectangle.** The 1 m path adds 1 m at each end of both dimensions. The outer length is 22 m and the outer width is 16 m.\
   `outer dimensions = 22 m × 16 m`
2. **Find both areas.** The outer rectangle covers 22 × 16 = 352 m². The garden covers 20 × 14 = 280 m².\
   `22 × 16 = 352 m² and 20 × 14 = 280 m²`
3. **Isolate the path.** The larger rectangle includes the garden and the path, so the path area is 352 − 280 = 72 m².\
   `352 − 280 = 72 m²`

**Answer:** 72 m².

#### Type 2 · Annular sector

**Question.** A 90° sector has outer radius 10 cm and inner radius 6 cm removed. Find remaining area.

**Picture it.** Picture a quarter of a thick ring. Find the area of the whole ring by removing its hole, then keep only the quarter shown.

1. **Find the full ring area.** The removed inner circle has radius 6 cm and the outside radius is 10 cm. The difference in their areas is π(10² − 6²) = 64π cm².\
   `π(10² − 6²) = 64π cm²`
2. **Find the sector share.** A 90° sector takes 90/360 = 1/4 of the ring. Both boundary circles use the same angle.\
   `90/360 = 1/4`
3. **Apply the fraction.** The remaining annular sector has area 64π × 1/4 = 16π cm².\
   `64π ÷ 4 = 16π cm²`

**Answer:** 16π cm².

### Method B · List physical surfaces before adding

An open-top cylinder uses one circular base and one curved wall. A closed one uses two bases. A cone has slant height along its surface but perpendicular height in its volume formula.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Mensuration / Surface Area** questions when the question asks how much material, paint or covering is needed and tells you which faces are open or closed.

**Key rule.** For an open-top cylinder, include πr² for the base plus 2πrh for the curved wall.

#### Type 1 · Open cylindrical container

**Question.** An open-top cylinder has radius 7 cm and height 10 cm. How much sheet area is needed, ignoring overlaps?

**Picture it.** Imagine making a round bin from a flat sheet. You need one round bottom and a strip that wraps around the side. The open top needs no piece.

1. **Identify the sheet pieces.** The container needs the cylindrical wall and one circular base. The opening at the top requires no sheet.\
   `total = wall + one base`
2. **Find the curved wall area.** Unrolling the wall gives a rectangle of width 2πr and height h. Its area is 2π × 7 × 10 = 140π cm².\
   `2π × 7 × 10 = 140π cm²`
3. **Add the base.** One circular base adds π × 7² = 49π cm². Total sheet area is 189π cm².\
   `140π + π × 7² = 189π cm²`

**Answer:** 189π cm².

### Method C · Convert volume before dividing by area

When a container has a uniform cross-section, height of a layer is volume divided by base area. Use 1 L = 1000 cm³ to combine litre amounts with centimetre dimensions.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Mensuration / Liquid Depth** questions when a volume of liquid is poured into a container with a constant cross-section and you need its new height.

**Key rule.** For a uniform cross-section, filled height = volume ÷ base area. Convert 1 L to 1,000 cm³ when using cm² for the base.

#### Type 1 · Transfer and capacity

**Question.** A tank base is 80 by 50 cm. Pour in 24 L. How far does the water level rise?

**Picture it.** Picture the new water as a flat layer over the whole tank floor. The same water would make a thinner layer over a bigger floor.

1. **Match the volume units.** One litre is 1,000 cm³. The added water therefore occupies 24 × 1,000 = 24,000 cm³.\
   `24 L = 24,000 cm³`
2. **Calculate the horizontal base area.** The rectangular base covers 80 × 50 = 4,000 cm². This is also the area of each horizontal layer of water.\
   `80 × 50 = 4,000 cm²`
3. **Find the layer’s height.** Volume gained is base area × rise. Divide 24,000 cm³ by 4,000 cm² to get a rise of 6 cm.\
   `24,000 ÷ 4,000 = 6 cm`

**Answer:** 6 cm.

#### Type 2 · Comparing solids in the same space

**Question.** A cone and a cylinder have radius 3 cm and height 8 cm. What volume is unfilled if the cone’s contents are poured into the cylinder?

**Picture it.** Imagine a cone and a straight cup with the same round floor and height. The cone holds one out of three parts of what the straight cup holds.

1. **Find the common base area.** Both solids have radius 3 cm, giving base area π × 3² = 9π cm².\
   `π × 3² = 9π cm²`
2. **Compare their volumes.** The cylinder holds base area × height = 9π × 8 = 72π cm³. The matching cone holds one-third of that, or 24π cm³.\
   `cylinder = 72π cm³, cone = 24π cm³`
3. **Subtract what is poured in.** The space left in the cylinder is 72π − 24π = 48π cm³.\
   `72π − 24π = 48π cm³`

**Answer:** 48π cm³.

## Exam tips

- **Read open, closed and filled carefully.** Surface area and capacity ask for different combinations.
- **Match area and volume units.** Dividing cm³ by cm² gives cm for the layer height.
- **Keep π symbolic.** Substitute a numerical value only if the question or choices require it.
