## What it means

A scatter plot shows pairs of numeric measurements as points. Upward or downward trend describes association between variables. Strength describes how tightly points follow a trend, independent of whether the slope looks steep. An outlier lies away from the pattern or differs substantially from a fitted prediction.

## Formulas and rules

- **Direction:** Positive means larger x generally pairs with larger y; negative means the reverse. A curved pattern can have a clear relationship even when a straight line fits poorly.
- **Rate of a fitted line:** Use its rise/run. A steep line can still have dispersed points, and units affect apparent slope.
- **Residual:** observed y−predicted y at the same x. A point above the line has positive residual.
- **Limits:** Use a trend for cautious estimates within the observed x range. An association alone cannot identify a causal effect.

## How to study it for Station Master

- Describe the pattern as direction, shape, spread and unusual points. Then check which of these the question actually asks for.
- Practise locating an outlier relative to a trend line and identifying a confounding variable. Compare only coordinates with the axis scales in view.

## Question models and worked examples

### Read direction and form

**Question.** Points are near (1,9), (2,7), (3,6), (4,4), (5,3). Describe the pattern.

1. As x increases, y broadly falls.
2. Successive decreases are roughly similar, so a descending line is a reasonable description.

**Answer:** Negative, roughly linear association.

### Residual and outlier

**Question.** A trend line predicts 22 faults at x=8. The plotted value is 35. Find the residual and interpret its sign.

1. Subtract prediction from observation: 35−22.
2. The positive sign means this point lies above the fitted line. Check other residuals before deciding how exceptional it is.

**Answer:** +13 faults.

### Slope and interpolation

**Question.** A fitted straight line passes through (2,15) and (6,27). Estimate y at x=4.

1. Rise/run=(27−15)/(6−2)=3 per unit of x.
2. Move two units right from x=2, so add 6 to 15.

**Answer:** 21.

### Correlation and causal claim

**Question.** Stores with more staff have higher daily sales. Does the scatter plot alone show that adding staff raises sales?

1. The plot records the association, with no controlled change.
2. Larger stores may both employ more staff and attract more buyers. That could explain the pattern.

**Answer:** No causal conclusion follows from the scatter plot alone.
