## What it means

A distribution describes how values are spread out, often through a frequency table, histogram or smooth curve. Frequency counts observations in a group, while cumulative frequency counts observations up to a boundary. Skew describes an extended tail. A density curve uses areas to represent proportions of a population.

## Formulas and rules

- **Frequency and cumulative:** Total count=Σf. To extract a class count from running totals, subtract successive cumulative counts.
- **Histogram:** If heights are frequency densities, frequency=height×class width. This matters when interval widths differ.
- **Shape:** Right skew has a long right tail, left skew a long left tail. A few large observations often pull the mean right in a right-skewed distribution, but direction of skew alone is safer than assuming an exact ordering.
- **Density curves:** Total area is 1. With a symmetric distribution centred at c, equal mirrored intervals have equal areas.

## How to study it for Station Master

- Read the vertical axis before deciding whether to compare heights, areas or cumulative gaps. For median classes, locate the central rank, then identify which band holds it.
- Practise detecting a tail from several bins. Avoid treating the tallest bin as the centre or the direction of skew automatically.

## Question models and worked examples

### Unequal-width histogram

**Question.** A band of width 5 has frequency density 4. Another of width 10 has density 3. Which has more readings?

1. First area = 5×4=20 readings.
2. Second area = 10×3=30 readings. Comparing bar heights alone would reverse the answer.

**Answer:** The second band, by 10.

### Recover a class and median band

**Question.** Cumulative counts below 10, 20, 30 and 40 are 8, 21, 37 and 48. Find the count from 20 inclusive to 30 exclusive and the median band.

1. The 20–30 class adds 37−21=16 observations.
2. Middle positions 24 and 25 lie after the first 21 and within the first 37.

**Answer:** 16 observations. Median band is 20–30.

### Recognise skew

**Question.** Most times lie around 10–15 minutes, but increasingly sparse values stretch as far as 60 minutes on the high side. What shape is indicated?

1. Look at the extended sparse tail rather than just the common range.
2. The tail stretches toward larger values, which corresponds to the right on a standard axis.

**Answer:** Right-skewed.

### Symmetric curve area

**Question.** A continuous symmetric distribution centred at 50 has 18% of its area between 50 and 60. What share lies between 40 and 60?

1. 40–50 is the mirror of 50–60 about the centre.
2. Equal areas of 18% add across the two halves.

**Answer:** 36%.
