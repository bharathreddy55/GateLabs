A distribution shows how values or probabilities are spread. Histograms group quantitative observations into intervals. For unequal class widths, use frequency density so area represents frequency. For a probability density curve, an interval's probability is the area under that interval.

## Concepts in depth

A frequency distribution groups equal values or intervals. Sum frequencies for sample size, use cumulative frequency for rank or median, and weight each value in the mean. For grouped intervals, a midpoint mean is generally an estimate.

## How to apply it

If waiting times 5, 10 and 15 minutes have frequencies 2, 3 and 5, total time is 115 minutes and mean is 11.5. Median averages observations 5 and 6, which here are 10 and 15. Frequency density is needed for histograms with unequal widths.

## Worked example

If 20 observations fall in a bin 5 units wide, its frequency density is 4 observations per unit. A bin twice as wide can have the same height and twice the frequency.

## Common mistake

A density curve's height at a value differs from the probability of a range. Use area for the range.

## Practice

What does a long right tail indicate?

**Answer:** The distribution is right-skewed, with a relatively small number of large values extending to the right.
