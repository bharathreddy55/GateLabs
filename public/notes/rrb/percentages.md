## What it means

A percentage is a fraction of a named base expressed out of 100. A change in a rate can be described as an absolute difference in percentage points or a relative change as a percentage of the original rate. The correct calculation depends on the wording.

## Formulas and rules

- **Part, rate, base:** Part=(p/100)×base. Divide by p/100 to recover a base from a known part.
- **Change:** (new−old)/old ×100%, using a nonzero old value.
- **Multipliers:** Rise p% → multiply by 1+p/100. Fall p% → multiply by 1−p/100. Multiply factors for successive changes.
- **Recovery after a fall:** After falling p%, a rise of 100p/(100−p)% restores the original, provided p<100.

## How to study it for Station Master

- Underline “of”, “than” and “original” to find the denominator. Use 100 as a starting value only if an actual starting magnitude is unnecessary.
- Mix relative change, percentage-point change and share of total in one drill. Estimate the direction before calculating.

## Question models and worked examples

### Reverse percentage

**Question.** After an 18% increase daily output is 354 units. What was it before?

1. The final value is 1.18 times the old value.
2. 354/1.18=300. Check that 18% of 300 is 54.

**Answer:** 300 units.

### Successive changes

**Question.** Usage rises 25% then falls 20%. Find the net change.

1. Start at 100. The increase gives 125.
2. Apply 20% to 125, so usage falls by 25 and returns to 100.

**Answer:** 0%.

### Percentage points versus percent

**Question.** A rejection rate goes from 4% to 5%. Give both changes.

1. Subtract percentages for a difference of one percentage point.
2. Relative change is (5−4)/4×100%=25%.

**Answer:** Up 1 percentage point, or 25% relative to the old rate.

### Fixed budget

**Question.** Price rises 12.5%. Find the percent reduction in quantity affordable under the same budget, allowing fractional quantities.

1. The price multiplier is 9/8, so quantity multiplies by 8/9.
2. The reduction is 1−8/9=1/9 of the old quantity.

**Answer:** 11⅑% fewer.
