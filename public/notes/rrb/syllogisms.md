Translate all, no, and some into set relationships. Universal premises constrain membership but do not establish an instance under ordinary modern logic. An existential premise supplies at least one instance. Use a counterexample to test a conclusion.

## Worked example

Some A are B. No B are C. Hence some A are not C, because the A that is B cannot be C.

## Common mistake

All A are B does not imply all B are A. An example within the larger set might lie outside A.

## Practice

All trains are vehicles. No buses are trains. Must no buses be vehicles?

**Answer:** No. Buses can be vehicles that are outside the train subset.
