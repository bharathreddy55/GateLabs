## Basics first

- **Lever and pivot.** A lever rotates about a fulcrum. A force has a moment equal to force times its perpendicular distance from the pivot’s line of action.
- **Lever classes.** Class I puts the fulcrum between effort and load. Class II puts the load in the middle. Class III puts effort in the middle.
- **Mechanical advantage and velocity ratio.** MA compares load with effort. Velocity ratio compares distances moved by effort and load. A machine can reduce force by making effort travel farther.
- **Efficiency.** Useful work divided by input work gives efficiency. In the models here, losses make actual MA smaller than ideal MA at the same velocity ratio.

## Methods and when to use them

### Method A · Balance turning effects

Draw the fulcrum and direction of each force. At equilibrium the clockwise moments equal anticlockwise moments. Multiply by the perpendicular arm rather than any slanted length from the pivot.

### Method B · Compare force and distance ratios

Use MA = load/effort and VR = effort distance/load distance. Efficiency = MA/VR when the force distances align as in the question. For a ramp, effort distance is along the ramp while the load’s useful rise is vertical.

### Method C · Track supporting strands in a simple pulley

When n rope segments supporting the moving block each shorten by the rise h, the free end must move nh. Multiply free-end movement by effort for input work and load rise by load for useful work.

## Question models and worked examples

### Lever moments

**Question.** A 300 N load acts 0.2 m from the pivot. How much perpendicular effort at 0.75 m on the opposite side balances it?

1. **Find the load’s turning moment.** Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot. The load produces 300 × 0.2 = 60 N·m.\
   `300 × 0.2 = 60 N·m`
2. **Write the balance condition.** The effort acts on the opposite side, so its moment must match 60 N·m. With effort E, the equation is E × 0.75 = 60.\
   `effort × 0.75 = 60`
3. **Solve for effort.** Divide 60 by the perpendicular arm of 0.75 m to get 80 N.\
   `effort = 60/0.75 = 80 N`

**Answer:** 80 N.

### Lever class from layout

**Question.** In a wheelbarrow the wheel axle is the fulcrum, load sits between axle and hands, and hands lift the handles. Classify it.

1. **Locate the three parts.** The wheel axle is the fulcrum, the load sits in the tray, and the hands supply effort at the handles.
2. **Read their order along the lever.** From the axle to the handles, the order is fulcrum → load → effort. The load is in the middle.
3. **Apply the classification.** A lever with its load between the fulcrum and effort is Class II.

**Answer:** Class II.

### Inclined plane efficiency

**Question.** A 240 N load rises 1 m along a 5 m ramp using 60 N along the ramp. Find MA and efficiency.

1. **Find the mechanical advantage.** Divide useful load by effort, MA = 240/60 = 4.\
   `MA = 240/60 = 4`
2. **Find the velocity ratio.** Effort moves along 5 m of ramp for a 1 m rise, so VR = 5/1 = 5.\
   `VR = 5/1 = 5`
3. **Calculate and check efficiency.** MA/VR = 4/5 = 80%. The work ratio gives the same result, 240 × 1 ÷ (60 × 5).\
   `η = 4/5 = 0.80 = 80%`

**Answer:** MA 4, efficiency 80%.

### Pulley with loss

**Question.** A moving block is supported by four rope segments. The 320 N load needs 100 N effort and rises 2 m. Find rope pulled and efficiency.

1. **Find the free-end movement.** Each of the four supporting segments shortens by 2 m when the moving block rises by 2 m. The free end must supply 4 × 2 = 8 m of movement.\
   `rope pulled = 4 × 2 = 8 m`
2. **Compare useful and supplied work.** Useful work is load × rise = 320 × 2 = 640 J. Supplied work is effort × pull distance = 100 × 8 = 800 J.\
   `useful work = 320 × 2 = 640 J`\
   `input work = 100 × 8 = 800 J`
3. **Calculate efficiency.** Divide useful work by supplied work, 640/800 = 0.8, or 80%.\
   `η = 640/800 = 80%`

**Answer:** 8 m pulled, efficiency 80%.

## Quick revision sheet

| When you see | Use this rule |
| --- | --- |
| Turning moment | Force × perpendicular distance. |
| Balanced lever | Load × load arm = effort × effort arm when moments oppose. |
| Mechanical advantage | Load force ÷ effort force. |
| Efficiency | Useful work ÷ input work, or MA ÷ VR under the given geometry. |

## Exam tips

- **Identify the middle component for class.** Use the order of effort, load and fulcrum.
- **Count only segments supporting the moving block.** A fixed pulley that merely changes pull direction may add no supporting segment.
- **Compare energy units.** A force gain alone does not imply an efficiency above 100%.
