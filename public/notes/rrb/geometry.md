## Basics first

- **Angle facts.** A straight angle is 180° and a full turn is 360°. The three interior angles of a plane triangle sum to 180°.
- **Exterior angle.** Extend one side of a triangle. The exterior angle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles and supplements the adjacent angle.
- **Similar figures.** Corresponding angles match and lengths have a common scale factor k. Area scales by k² because both dimensions change.
- **Tangent and radius.** At a point of tangency, the radius is perpendicular to the tangent line. An external point, the centre and the tangency point form a right triangle.

## Methods and when to use them

### Method A · Mark equal and supplementary angles

Draw the shape and label each stated angle. With parallel lines, use the corresponding or alternate interior relationships to find matching angles, then prove similarity by two angles.

### Method B · Turn an angle sum into shares

If two angles in ratio m:n add to S, one share is S/(m+n). In a regular polygon each exterior turn equals 180° minus one interior angle. The turns complete a full circuit.

### Method C · Identify the hypotenuse before using Pythagoras

The longest side lies opposite the right angle. At a tangent that side is the distance from the external point to the centre. Solve for the missing leg by subtracting squares.

## Question models and worked examples

### Exterior angle with a ratio

**Question.** A triangle’s exterior angle is 132°. Its remote interior angles are in ratio 5:6. Find the three interior angles.

1. **Use the exterior angle theorem.** The exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior angles away from its vertex. Those two angles therefore total 132°.\
   `A + B = 132°`
2. **Split the sum in ratio 5:6.** The 11 total parts give 132 ÷ 11 = 12° per part. The angles are 5 × 12° = 60° and 6 × 12° = 72°.\
   `132° ÷ (5 + 6) = 12° per share`\
   `(5, 6) × 12° = (60°, 72°)`
3. **Find the angle at the exterior vertex.** That angle and 132° form a straight line, so the interior angle is 180° − 132° = 48°. All three add to 180°.\
   `180° − 132° = 48°`

**Answer:** 60°, 72°, 48°.

### Parallel line and area

**Question.** In ABC, D is on AB and E on AC with DE parallel to BC. AD:DB=2:3. Area of ABC is 125 cm². Find area of ADE.

1. **Establish similarity.** DE is parallel to BC. The matching angles at D and B, together with the angle at A, make ADE similar to ABC.\
   `∠ADE = ∠ABC and ∠DAE = ∠BAC`
2. **Find the matching side ratio.** AB includes both AD and DB. From AD:DB = 2:3, AD/AB = 2/(2+3) = 2/5.\
   `AD/AB = 2/(2 + 3) = 2/5`
3. **Square the side scale for area.** Both dimensions scale by 2/5, so the area scale is 4/25. The smaller area is 125 × 4/25 = 20 cm².\
   `125 × (2/5)² = 20 cm²`

**Answer:** 20 cm².

### Regular polygon

**Question.** A regular polygon has an interior angle of 156°. Find its sides.

1. **Find the outside turn.** Each regular-polygon vertex has interior angle 156°. Its exterior turn is 180° − 156° = 24°.\
   `180° − 156° = 24°`
2. **Use one full circuit.** Walking around the polygon in one direction makes a complete turn of 360°. Each vertex contributes the same 24°.\
   `n × 24° = 360°`
3. **Count the vertices.** There are 360 ÷ 24 = 15 turns, giving 15 sides.\
   `n = 360/24 = 15`

**Answer:** 15.

### Tangent length

**Question.** Distance from external point P to circle centre O is 13 cm, with radius 5 cm. Find the length to a touchpoint T.

1. **Mark the right angle.** The radius OT is perpendicular to the tangent PT at T, so triangle OTP has its right angle at T.\
   `∠OTP = 90°`
2. **Apply Pythagoras.** OP is opposite the right angle, so it is the 13 cm hypotenuse. OT is the 5 cm radius. Thus PT² = 13² − 5² = 144.\
   `PT² = 13² − 5² = 144`
3. **Take the length.** The positive square root of 144 gives PT = 12 cm.\
   `PT = √144 = 12 cm`

**Answer:** 12 cm.

## Quick revision sheet

| When you see | Use this rule |
| --- | --- |
| Triangle exterior angle | Sum of the two remote interior angles. |
| Similar triangles at scale k | Lengths × k and areas × k². |
| Number of sides of a regular polygon | 360° ÷ (180° − interior angle). |
| Right triangle | Hypotenuse² = leg₁² + leg₂². |

## Exam tips

- **Do not trust the sketch’s apparent size.** Use given lengths, angle marks or derived relationships.
- **Identify the whole segment.** If AD:DB = 2:3, AD:AB is 2:5.
- **Use an exterior turn for a regular polygon.** Dividing 360° by its interior angle gives the wrong count.
