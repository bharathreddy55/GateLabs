## What it means

A linear equation uses variables to the first power with constant coefficients. Its solutions make the two sides equal. Two equations often represent different constraints, such as a total number and a total price. Solving preserves equality by doing the same operation to both sides.

## Formulas and rules

- **One variable:** ax+b=c gives x=(c−b)/a for nonzero a. Clear fractions by multiplying every term by their common denominator.
- **Two variables:** Substitution replaces one variable with a known expression. In elimination, align coefficients before adding or subtracting equations.
- **When variables cancel:** A true constant equation means the reduced equation adds no restriction. A false constant equation means the original constraints are inconsistent.

## How to study it for Station Master

- Translate words into equalities with units. “Four years ago” changes both ages. “Remaining tickets” uses total minus the other count.
- Mix age, cost, fraction and duplicate-equation questions. Put solved values in both original conditions and check counts are whole and nonnegative.

## Question models and worked examples

### Ages across years

**Question.** A is 8 years older than B. Four years ago A was twice B’s age. Find their present ages.

1. **Name the current ages.** Let B be x years old, so A is x + 8. Four years ago, B was x − 4 and A was x + 4.
2. **Translate “twice” into an equation.** The age condition gives x + 4 = 2(x − 4). Expanding yields x + 4 = 2x − 8, so x = 12.
3. **Check both clues.** A is 12 + 8 = 20. Four years ago they were 16 and 8, and 16 is twice 8.

**Answer:** A is 20, B is 12.

### Ticket count and price

**Question.** 54 tickets at ₹30 or ₹50 bring in ₹2,060. How many of each?

1. **Choose one unknown.** Let y be the number of ₹50 tickets. That leaves 54 − y tickets at ₹30.
2. **Use the revenue condition.** The two amounts add to 30(54 − y) + 50y = 2,060. Expanding gives 1,620 + 20y = 2,060.
3. **Solve and verify.** 20y = 440, so y = 22 and 54 − y = 32. Revenue is 22 × ₹50 + 32 × ₹30 = ₹2,060.

**Answer:** 32 at ₹30 and 22 at ₹50.

### Clear fractions

**Question.** Solve (x−2)/3+(x+1)/4=7.

1. **Clear the two denominators.** The least common multiple of 3 and 4 is 12. Multiply every term by 12 to obtain 4(x−2) + 3(x+1) = 84.
2. **Expand and collect.** The left side is 4x − 8 + 3x + 3 = 7x − 5. Therefore 7x = 89.
3. **Divide to solve.** x = 89/7. Substitution gives (25/7) + (24/7) = 49/7 = 7 in the original equation.

**Answer:** 89/7.

### Dependent equations

**Question.** Do x+y=12 and 3x+3y=36 determine x and y uniquely?

1. **Compare the two equations.** Dividing every term of 3x + 3y = 36 by 3 gives x + y = 12, the first equation again.
2. **Describe the possible pairs.** Choose any real x, and set y = 12 − x. This gives a pair satisfying both equations.
3. **Check that multiple pairs qualify.** For example, (0,12) and (5,7) both work. The second equation supplies no independent constraint.

**Answer:** No. There are infinitely many real solutions.
