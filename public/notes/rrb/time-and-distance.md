## Basics first

- **Speed, distance and time.** Speed measures distance covered per unit time. A train running at 54 km/h covers 54 km in an hour at that constant speed.
- **Units.** The factor from km/h to m/s is 1000/3600 = 5/18. Use metres and seconds when the question gives a train length in metres.
- **Relative speed.** The closing rate is how fast a gap shrinks. Same-direction speeds subtract if the faster object follows. Opposite-direction speeds add.
- **Average speed.** Divide the whole distance by elapsed time. A stop contributes zero distance and still uses time.

## Methods and when to use them

### Method A · Draw the complete crossing

A train clears a point once its rear reaches that point, so it travels its full length. To clear a platform, the front travels train length plus platform length. Two trains passing each other have to clear both lengths.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Time, Speed and Distance / Train Crossings** questions when a train must completely pass a pole, platform, bridge or another train.

### Method B · Start the catch-up clock when both are moving

Calculate the lead before the second vehicle starts. Divide this gap by the closing speed. Add that time to the later departure. If the follower is no faster, the gap does not close under constant speeds.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Time, Speed and Distance / Catch-up Problems** questions when two travellers move in the same direction and one gets a head start before the other leaves.

### Method C · Build a timeline for average speed

For each segment compute time = distance ÷ speed. Convert minutes to hours or vice versa before adding. Divide the total distance by the sum of all times, including specified stops.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Time, Speed and Distance / Average Speed** questions when the question asks for the speed over a complete trip with several legs, different speeds or specified stops.

## Question models and worked examples

### Train and platform

**Question.** A 180 m train at 54 km/h clears a platform in 22 seconds. Find the platform length.

**Picture it.** Watch the front and back of the train. It has cleared the platform only when the back passes the far end. The front has to move an extra train length.

1. **Convert the speed.** Multiply 54 km/h by 5/18 to get 15 m/s. This matches the metres and seconds in the problem.\
   `54 × 5/18 = 15 m/s`
2. **Find how far the train moves.** From the front entering to the rear exiting, the train moves past the entire platform plus its own length. In 22 seconds it travels 15 × 22 = 330 m.\
   `15 × 22 = 330 m`
3. **Subtract the train length.** The platform contributes 330 − 180 = 150 m to the total clearing distance.\
   `330 − 180 = 150 m`

**Answer:** 150 m.

### Delayed catch-up

**Question.** A truck leaves at 08:00 at 45 km/h. A van leaves at 08:40 at 65 km/h. When does it catch the truck?

**Picture it.** Picture the truck already ahead when the van starts. From then on, every hour removes only the difference between their speeds from that gap.

1. **Find the truck’s head start.** Before the van leaves, the truck travels for 40 minutes, or 2/3 of an hour. Its lead is 45 × 2/3 = 30 km.\
   `40 min = 2/3 h`\
   `45 × 2/3 = 30 km`
2. **Find the closing speed.** Both vehicles travel in the same direction. The lead shrinks by 65 − 45 = 20 km each hour.\
   `65 − 45 = 20 km/h`
3. **Count from the later start.** Closing the 30 km gap takes 30 ÷ 20 = 1.5 hours. Add 1 hour 30 minutes to 08:40 to get 10:10.\
   `30 ÷ 20 = 1.5 h`\
   `08:40 + 1:30 = 10:10`

**Answer:** 10:10.

### Average speed with a stop

**Question.** A bus covers 72 km at 48 km/h and stops for 15 minutes. Find trip average speed.

**Picture it.** Imagine the bus's clock still ticking during the stop. Divide the total trip distance by everything the clock recorded, including those minutes.

1. **Calculate driving time.** The bus takes 72 ÷ 48 = 1.5 hours to cover the distance while moving.\
   `72 ÷ 48 = 1.5 h`
2. **Include the stop.** Fifteen minutes is 0.25 hour, so the whole trip lasts 1.5 + 0.25 = 1.75 hours.\
   `15/60 = 0.25 h`\
   `1.5 + 0.25 = 1.75 h`
3. **Use elapsed time.** Average speed is 72 ÷ 1.75 = 288/7 km/h. This is about 41.14 km/h, below the moving speed of 48.\
   `72 ÷ 1.75 ≈ 41.14 km/h`

**Answer:** About 41.14 km/h.

### Passing trains

**Question.** Trains of 150 m and 210 m approach at 72 and 36 km/h. How long from nose-to-nose to fully clear?

**Picture it.** Imagine two long trains sliding past each other. Until the back ends are clear, their full lengths must pass. Because both trains move, they close the gap together.

1. **Add the approaching speeds.** The trains move towards each other, so relative speed is 72 + 36 = 108 km/h, or 30 m/s.\
   `(72 + 36) × 5/18 = 30 m/s`
2. **Add the lengths to be cleared.** After the fronts meet, the rears must also pass. The required relative travel is 150 + 210 = 360 m.\
   `150 + 210 = 360 m`
3. **Divide distance by speed.** The crossing lasts 360 ÷ 30 = 12 seconds.\
   `360 ÷ 30 = 12 s`

**Answer:** 12 seconds.

## Quick revision sheet

| When you see | Use this rule |
| --- | --- |
| Convert km/h to m/s | Multiply by 5/18. Reverse by multiplying by 18/5. |
| Clear a platform | Time = (train length + platform length) ÷ speed. |
| Opposite motion | Relative speed = sum of speed magnitudes. |
| Same-direction catch-up | Time after follower starts = head start ÷ speed difference. |

## Exam tips

- **Identify the endpoints.** “Fully clears” is a rear-end event.
- **Put both speeds in the same unit.** Do the conversion before adding or subtracting.
- **Separate elapsed time and clock time.** Add the elapsed catch-up interval only to the later start.
