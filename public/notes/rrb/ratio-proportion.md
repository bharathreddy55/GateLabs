## Basics first

- **Ratio.** A ratio compares quantities by division after they are in compatible units. A 3:2 split means three shares for one part and two for the other.
- **Proportion.** Two ratios are proportional when they represent the same quotient. In a:b = c:d, cross-products satisfy ad = bc if the denominators are nonzero.
- **Direct and inverse variation.** With a constant daily rate, more days produce proportionally more output. For fixed output, multiplying the workforce divides the time by the same factor.
- **Part to part and part to whole.** A 3:2 ratio makes the first part 3/5 of the whole. For mixtures, concentration is solute amount divided by total mixture amount.

## Methods and when to use them

### Method A · Scale the common term

In linked ratios align the shared quantity before stitching the ratios together. For 3:4 and 6:5, make the shared value 12 by scaling the first pair by 3 and the second by 2.

### Method B · Turn shares into an equation

Write stocks in a:b as ak and bk. If a transfer moves x from first to second, subtract x from one and add it to the other. Their total stays fixed and the gap changes by 2x.

### Method C · Use a per-unit rate or weighted share

For variable workers and time, compute output per worker per day, then scale to the requested staffing and output. In a mixture, compute solute in each input and combine, using mass weights for mass concentration.

## Question models and worked examples

### Linked ratios

**Question.** A:B=3:4 and B:C=6:5. Split ₹1,860 between them.

1. **Match B in both ratios.** B represents 4 parts in A:B and 6 in B:C. Their least common multiple is 12. Multiply the first ratio by 3 and the second by 2 to get A:B = 9:12 and B:C = 12:10.\
   `3:4 = 9:12 and 6:5 = 12:10`
2. **Find the value of a part.** The combined ratio is 9:12:10, so the full amount is 31 parts. Divide ₹1,860 by 31 to get ₹60 per part.\
   `9 + 12 + 10 = 31 shares`\
   `₹1,860 ÷ 31 = ₹60`
3. **Allocate the amount.** Multiply ₹60 by 9, 12 and 10. The resulting shares add back to ₹1,860.\
   `(9, 12, 10) × ₹60 = (₹540, ₹720, ₹600)`

**Answer:** ₹540, ₹720, ₹600.

### Ratio after a transfer

**Question.** Stocks are in ratio 7:5. Transfer 24 units from the first to the second and they become equal. Find the originals.

1. **Track the original stocks.** Write the stocks as 7k and 5k. A transfer of 24 changes the first to 7k − 24 and the second to 5k + 24.\
   `7k and 5k`
2. **Equate the new amounts.** Solve 7k − 24 = 5k + 24. This gives 2k = 48 and k = 24.\
   `7k − 24 = 5k + 24 ⇒ 2k = 48 ⇒ k = 24`
3. **Recover and verify the originals.** Calculate 7 × 24 = 168 and 5 × 24 = 120. After transferring 24, both become 144.\
   `(7, 5) × 24 = (168, 120)`

**Answer:** 168 and 120.

### Inverse and direct variation

**Question.** Eight workers mark 120 m in six days. How many days for 12 workers to mark 210 m at the same output per worker?

1. **Find the daily rate per worker.** Eight workers over six days supply 48 worker-days. At the same output per person, one worker-day produces 120 ÷ 48 = 2.5 m.\
   `8 × 6 = 48 worker-days`
2. **Convert the new job to worker-days.** At 2.5 m per worker-day, marking 210 m takes 210 ÷ 2.5 = 84 worker-days.\
   `120 ÷ 48 = 2.5 m per worker-day`
3. **Share the work across 12 workers.** The 12 workers supply 12 worker-days each calendar day, so the job takes 84 ÷ 12 = 7 days.\
   `210 ÷ (12 × 2.5) = 7 days`

**Answer:** 7 days.

### Weighted mixture

**Question.** Mix 30% and 50% solutions in a 3:2 mass ratio. Find the resulting concentration.

1. **Choose a convenient total mass.** Take 3 kg of the 30% mixture and 2 kg of the 50% mixture. The ratio is by mass, so the combined mass is 5 kg.\
   `3 × 0.30 = 0.90 kg`
2. **Add the solute from each portion.** The first contributes 3 × 0.30 = 0.9 kg and the second contributes 2 × 0.50 = 1 kg, giving 1.9 kg of solute.\
   `2 × 0.50 = 1.00 kg`
3. **Divide by the whole mixture.** Concentration by mass is solute mass ÷ total mass × 100%. Here 1.9 ÷ 5 × 100% = 38%.\
   `(0.90 + 1.00) ÷ 5 × 100% = 38%`

**Answer:** 38%.

## Quick revision sheet

| When you see | Use this rule |
| --- | --- |
| Divide a sum in a:b | One share = total ÷ (a + b). |
| Linked ratios | Match the common quantity before using a combined share count. |
| Transfer to make equal | If moving from the larger amount to the smaller, transfer half their initial difference. |
| Constant productivity | Output = workers × days × output per worker-day. |
| Concentration on one basis | Combined concentration = total solute ÷ total mixture. |

## Exam tips

- **Normalise first.** Convert metres and centimetres before forming a ratio of lengths.
- **Watch inverse variation.** More workers decrease time only when the required work is held fixed.
- **Verify what stayed constant.** A transfer preserves the total. A ratio comparison preserves the common scale.
