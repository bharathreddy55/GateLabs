## What it means

A bar chart compares sizes across categories. Grouped bars show several separate series for each category. In a stacked chart, a band’s value is the difference between its upper and lower edges, and the full stack shows the total when the axis uses counts. A 100% stacked chart instead shows proportions.

## Formulas and rules

- **Read the scale:** Verify the axis origin, grid increment, unit and legend. Compare values at bar endpoints.
- **Grouped bars:** Difference within a category uses two series. Growth across periods uses the same series and starts from its earlier value.
- **Stacked bars:** Segment=upper cumulative boundary−lower boundary. If all bars are normalised to 100%, recover counts only when category totals are given.
- **Share:** Segment value/appropriate group total ×100%.

## How to study it for Station Master

- Label series with short identifiers and record readings before comparing. Watch for the switch between comparing a stack total and comparing one component.
- Practise comparing segments in unequal stacks and bars on a truncated axis. Estimate how many grid intervals each value spans.

## Question models and worked examples

### Grouped comparison

**Question.** Dispatch bars for Monday are 28 express and 42 local. Tuesday shows 33 express and 35 local. Which day has the higher express share?

1. Monday’s share is 28/(28+42)=40%.
2. Tuesday’s is 33/(33+35)=33/68, about 48.5%.

**Answer:** Tuesday.

### Read a stacked band

**Question.** In a count stack, the first band ends at 180, the second ends at 315 and the top ends at 420. Find the second band’s share.

1. Its own height is 315−180=135.
2. Divide by the stack total 420 to get 9/28.

**Answer:** About 32.14%.

### Normalised stack

**Question.** Digital shares are 40% at A and 35% at B. Totals are 300 and 480. Which has more digital transactions?

1. A: 0.40×300=120.
2. B: 0.35×480=168. A longer percentage band can still represent fewer transactions.

**Answer:** B, by 48.

### Axis starting above zero

**Question.** One bar ends at 110 and the other at 130 on an axis beginning at 100. What is the actual percent increase from first to second?

1. Read actual endpoints as 110 and 130. The plotted lengths above the axis are 10 and 30.
2. Divide the real increase of 20 by the old value 110.

**Answer:** About 18.18%.
