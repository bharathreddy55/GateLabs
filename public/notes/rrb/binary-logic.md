A proposition has a truth value. NOT reverses it, AND needs both inputs true, and inclusive OR needs at least one true. A truth table tests every input combination. For truth-teller puzzles, assign each claim true or false and reject inconsistent cases.

## Concepts in depth

AND requires both inputs, inclusive OR permits either or both, and exclusive OR requires exactly one. Implication is false only when its antecedent is true and consequent false. For truth-teller questions count true statements after substituting each possible case.

## How to apply it

If A and B make complementary claims, precisely one of those two claims is true under each case. With exactly two true statements overall, a third claim is forced true. This can remove the need for a full truth table.

## Worked example

P is true and Q false. P AND Q is false, P OR Q is true, and NOT Q is true.

## Common mistake

Ordinary OR in formal logic is inclusive unless the problem explicitly says exactly one.

## Practice

When is P XOR Q true?

**Answer:** Exactly when one of P and Q is true.
