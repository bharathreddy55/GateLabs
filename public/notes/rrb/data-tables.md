## What it means

A table encodes facts at the intersection of a row and column. A value may be a count, a rate, a total or a cumulative figure. The label and unit are part of the value. Before comparing entries, check whether both use the same period, population and scale.

## Formulas and rules

- **Difference:** Subtract comparable entries. **Percent change:** Divide the difference by the starting entry, then multiply by 100.
- **Part share:** Category count/total count ×100%. Choose the total for the group named in the question.
- **Combining rates:** Add numerators and denominators separately. If a table gives rates and totals, recover counts with rate×base first.
- **Cumulative entries:** Subtract the earlier running total from the later one to get the amount added over that interval.

## How to study it for Station Master

- Write a small scratch table with only the relevant cells and their units. Circle the denominator before doing any percentage work.
- Practise a reverse percentage and an overall rate. In timed sets, rule out options through estimation when the columns use large scales.

## Question models and worked examples

### Column comparison and percent change

**Question.** Passenger counts in April and May were 3200 and 3680 at A, and 2800 and 3110 at B. Which station grew faster by percentage?

1. A gained 480 on 3200, a rise of 15%.
2. B gained 310 on 2800, about 11.1%. Each station has its own base.

**Answer:** A.

### Overall rate from rows

**Question.** Morning: 240 trains, 90% on time. Evening: 160 trains, 75% on time. Find overall punctuality.

1. Convert to counts: 216 on time in morning and 120 in evening.
2. Divide 336 on-time trains by 400 total. A simple mean of the two rates would weight the shifts equally.

**Answer:** 84%.

### Find a missing row count

**Question.** A table lists 120 service requests on Monday, 140 on Tuesday and a three-day average of 135. Find Wednesday’s count.

1. The total over three days must be 3×135=405.
2. Subtract Monday and Tuesday’s total of 260.

**Answer:** 145.

### Cumulative figures

**Question.** A running log shows 780 tickets by noon, 1120 by 2 pm, and 1620 by 5 pm. What share of tickets sold from noon to 5 pm came after 2 pm?

1. The 2–5 pm interval added 1620−1120=500 tickets.
2. The noon–5 pm interval added 1620−780=840. Compare 500 with 840.

**Answer:** 25/42, about 59.5%.
