## Basics first

- **Distance and displacement.** Distance adds the lengths of all segments. Displacement is final position minus initial position and has a direction or sign.
- **Speed and velocity.** Speed is a magnitude. Average velocity uses displacement divided by time, and average speed uses distance divided by the same time.
- **Acceleration.** Acceleration is change of velocity per unit time. A negative sign means acceleration points in the negative direction chosen for the problem.
- **Relative velocity.** Velocity relative to an observer is object velocity minus observer velocity. Assign a direction as positive before subtracting.

## Methods and when to use them

### Method A · Label every motion with a sign

Write east or right as positive and the opposite as negative. Add signed segments to get displacement. For distance, add their absolute lengths.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Velocity and Motion / Distance and Displacement** questions when successive moves go in opposite directions and you need the final position, displacement or total distance.

### Method B · Subtract the observer’s velocity

In “B relative to A,” take vB − vA. Check by imagining A stationary after subtracting vA from both objects. This also works for objects travelling in opposite directions.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Relative Velocity / Moving Observers** questions when asked for one object’s motion as seen by another moving person or vehicle.

### Method C · Use a velocity–time graph or constant acceleration

A graph’s signed area gives displacement. Sum magnitudes for distance when the sign switches. At constant acceleration, the mean of initial and final velocities gives signed displacement when multiplied by time.

**When to use it.** Use this in **Velocity and Motion / Graphs and Constant Acceleration** questions when a velocity–time graph or constant acceleration is given and you need distance or displacement over a time interval.

## Question models and worked examples

### Average speed and velocity

**Question.** Run 80 m east then 50 m west in 26 s. Find the average speed and velocity.

**Picture it.** You walk along both parts of the road, but the walk back undoes some of the first change in position. Distance counts both and displacement counts how far from the start you end up.

1. **Add the path lengths.** Distance counts all travel. It is 80 + 50 = 130 m, so average speed is 130/26 = 5 m/s.\
   `distance = 80 + 50 = 130 m`\
   `130/26 = 5 m/s`
2. **Find the direction and displacement.** Using east as positive, displacement is 80 − 50 = 30 m east.\
   `displacement = 80 − 50 = +30 m`
3. **Divide the displacement by elapsed time.** Average velocity is 30/26 = 15/13 m/s east. Its magnitude is smaller because the westward leg cancels some displacement.\
   `30/26 = 15/13 m/s east`

**Answer:** 5 m/s speed and 15/13 m/s east velocity.

### Signed relative velocity

**Question.** A travels east at 18 m/s and B west at 12 m/s. What is B’s velocity relative to A?

**Picture it.** Imagine watching B while riding with A. Pretend your own ride is standing still by taking A's motion away from both riders. Work out which way B seems to move.

1. **Pick a sign convention.** Let east be positive. A’s velocity is +18 m/s and B’s is −12 m/s.\
   `vA = +18 m/s, vB = −12 m/s`
2. **Use the observer first.** B relative to A means subtract the observer A’s velocity from B’s velocity. The calculation is vB − vA = −12 − 18 = −30 m/s.\
   `−12 − (+18) = −30 m/s`
3. **Interpret the sign.** Negative represents west in our convention, so B moves west at 30 m/s relative to A.\
   `−30 m/s ⇒ 30 m/s west`

**Answer:** 30 m/s west.

### Constant braking

**Question.** A vehicle slows uniformly from 20 m/s to rest in 5 s. Find acceleration and distance.

**Picture it.** Picture a speed display falling evenly from 20 to zero. It spends the same amount of time above and below its middle speed, so the average gives you the distance.

1. **Find the signed acceleration.** Choose the original direction as positive. Acceleration is (final velocity − initial velocity) ÷ time = (0 − 20)/5 = −4 m/s².\
   `a = (0 − 20)/5 = −4 m/s²`
2. **Find the mean velocity during the interval.** Uniform braking makes velocity decline linearly, so its mean is (20 + 0) ÷ 2 = 10 m/s.\
   `(20 + 0)/2 = 10 m/s`
3. **Multiply by elapsed time.** It keeps moving forward until it stops, so distance equals this displacement, 10 × 5 = 50 m.\
   `10 × 5 = 50 m`

**Answer:** −4 m/s² and 50 m.

### Signed area of a velocity graph

**Question.** An object moves at +4 m/s for 3 s and then −2 m/s for 5 s. Find displacement and distance.

**Picture it.** Draw one arrow forward for the first part and another backward for the second. The distance counts both arrow lengths while displacement subtracts the backward part.

1. **Find the signed area of the first segment.** Velocity is +4 m/s for 3 s, giving an area of +4 × 3 = +12 m on the velocity–time graph.\
   `(+4) × 3 = +12 m`
2. **Find the signed area of the second segment.** Velocity is −2 m/s for 5 s, giving −2 × 5 = −10 m. The negative sign indicates reversed motion.\
   `(−2) × 5 = −10 m`
3. **Use the right kind of sum.** Displacement is 12 − 10 = +2 m. Distance counts both lengths, so it is 12 + 10 = 22 m.\
   `displacement = 12 − 10 = 2 m`\
   `distance = 12 + 10 = 22 m`

**Answer:** +2 m displacement, 22 m distance.

## Quick revision sheet

| When you see | Use this rule |
| --- | --- |
| Average speed | Total distance ÷ total time. |
| Average velocity | Displacement ÷ total time. |
| Relative velocity of B seen from A | vB − vA. |
| Uniform acceleration | a = (v − u)/t. Displacement = (u + v)t/2. |

## Exam tips

- **State the sign convention.** Explain what a negative result means in direction terms.
- **Check whether the velocity crosses zero.** If it does, split the distance calculation at that point.
- **Keep rate definitions distinct.** Velocity over time is acceleration only when using the change in velocity.
