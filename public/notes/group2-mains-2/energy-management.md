Installed capacity is rated power and is expressed in watts and their multiples. Electricity generated over time is energy, expressed in watt-hours or joules. Demand peaks and variability require grid planning, transmission, storage and flexibility. Compare coal and other fossil sources with solar, wind, hydro and bioenergy by resource availability, dispatchability, life-cycle effects and policy. Installed capacity share can differ considerably from generation share.

Biofuel policy concerns feedstocks, blending, supply chains and sustainability. Bharat Stage standards target specified emissions from road vehicles. Keep the standard stage, date and vehicle category separate. Use the Central Electricity Authority for dated power-sector totals and the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy for programme details and capacity series.

## Worked example

A 100 MW plant delivering 30 MW on average for 24 hours generates 720 MWh. Nameplate capacity remains 100 MW.

## Common mistake

Use MW for power and MWh for energy. Do not compare the numerical values directly.

## Practice

What does an installed capacity of 100 MW describe?

**Answer:** MW is a rate of energy production, while generation over time is measured in MWh.
