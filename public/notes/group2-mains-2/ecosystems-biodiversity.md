An ecosystem includes organisms and physical conditions connected by energy flow and material cycling. Producers, consumers and decomposers perform distinct functions. Biodiversity includes genetic, species and ecosystem diversity. Hotspots combine high endemism with significant habitat loss under the applicable definition. Threats include habitat change, invasive species, overexploitation, pollution and climate change.

In-situ conservation protects organisms in natural settings. Ex-situ conservation uses settings such as seed banks and managed collections. Biosphere reserves support conservation, sustainable use and research through zones with different functions. CITES regulates international trade in listed species. The Convention on Biological Diversity concerns conservation, sustainable use and fair sharing of benefits. Review India's wildlife legislation, protected areas and dated conservation projects through official releases.

## Worked example

A protected wetland keeps species within their habitat, an in-situ approach. A seed bank holds material away from the original habitat, an ex-situ approach.

## Common mistake

CITES listing concerns trade controls. It is distinct from the basis of an IUCN risk assessment.

## Practice

Which agreement regulates international trade in listed wildlife specimens?

**Answer:** CITES regulates international trade in specimens of listed wild fauna and flora.
