## Concepts in depth

The production approach sums output less intermediate consumption, preventing double counting. The expenditure identity is C + I + G + (X − M), with G measuring government purchases of goods and services. The income approach adds compensation of employees, operating surplus, mixed income and the relevant net taxes. Starting from GVA at basic prices, add taxes on products and subtract subsidies on products to get GDP at market prices. Then subtract consumption of fixed capital for a net domestic measure or add net primary income from abroad for a gross national measure.

## Worked example

If nominal GDP rises 10% while a comparable GDP deflator rises 4%, approximate real growth using 1.10 ÷ 1.04 − 1, about 5.8%.

## Common mistake

Use a constant-price series for real growth and check the base year before comparing versions.
