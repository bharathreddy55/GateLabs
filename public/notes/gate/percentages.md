Convert p% to p/100. A rise by p% multiplies by 1+p/100 and a fall multiplies by 1−p/100. For successive changes, multiply the factors. Percentage-point change subtracts percentage values directly.

## Concepts in depth

Percentage change uses the starting quantity as denominator. A percentage-point change compares two rates by subtraction. Successive proportional changes multiply factors, so opposite percentage changes of equal size usually do not cancel.

## How to apply it

A 20% rise followed by a 20% fall gives 1.20×0.80=0.96, a 4% loss. If a price after 15% discount equals ₹680, recover the original with 680/0.85. A rate from 40% to 50% rises by 10 points or 25% relative.

## Worked example

A price increases by 20% and then falls by 20%. The multiplier is 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96. The final price is 4% below the original.

## Common mistake

Identify the denominator that defines the base. From 20% to 25% is a 5-percentage-point rise and a 25% relative increase.

## Practice

₹720 is 80% of what original amount?

**Answer:** ₹900, since 720 ÷ 0.80 = 900.
