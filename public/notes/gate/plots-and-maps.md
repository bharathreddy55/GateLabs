Identify coordinate axes, units, scale, legend and viewing perspective. A 3D plot can show how a third variable changes over a plane, through height, colour or contours. In maps, scale is necessary to infer distances, and adjacent contours of equal interval indicate a steeper slope where they are closer.

## Concepts in depth

Maps and plots encode space with projections, units and symbols. Read orientation, labelled axes, origin and scale. A section view shows one cut, while an orthographic projection can hide depth and overlapping features.

## How to apply it

At a map scale of 1:25,000, 4 cm represents 100,000 cm or 1 km. On unequal graph scales, visual slope alone cannot compare numerical rate across panels. Read the values and divide coordinate changes.

## Worked example

On a map at 1:25,000, 4 cm represents 100,000 cm, or 1 km. If contours bunch tightly along that route, horizontal distance alone understates the difficulty of the climb.

## Common mistake

Perspective can make equal bars or surfaces appear different. Read coordinates rather than visual size alone.

## Practice

What do closely spaced contours of the same elevation interval indicate?

**Answer:** A steep gradient, because elevation changes quickly across a short horizontal distance.
