Conditional probability P(A|B) uses B as the reference group and equals P(A∩B)/P(B) when P(B)>0. Compute P(B) by summing across disjoint exhaustive cases. Bayes' theorem reverses the condition using P(B|A)P(A)/P(B). Independence means conditioning leaves the probability unchanged.

## Concepts in depth

Conditional probability is P(A∩B)/P(B) when P(B)>0. For disjoint exhaustive states, compute the evidence probability by summing each state prior times its likelihood. Bayes reverses the direction of conditioning using those quantities.

## How to apply it

If prevalence is 1%, sensitivity 90% and false-positive rate 5%, the positive predictive value is .009/(.009+.0495)≈15.4%. A high detection chance for affected people is not a high chance of being affected given a positive result.

## Worked example

One in ten items is defective. A flag catches 90% of defects and flags 20% of good items. Among 100 expected items, 9 defective and 18 good items are flagged. Thus P(defective|flag)=9/27=1/3.

## Common mistake

Keep P(flag|defective) separate from P(defective|flag). The base rate matters.

## Practice

If A and B are independent with P(A)=0.4 and P(B)=0.5, find P(A∩B).

**Answer:** 0.2, multiplying the two probabilities.
