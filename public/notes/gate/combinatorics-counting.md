Use addition for disjoint choices, multiplication for stages, inclusion–exclusion for overlaps and pigeonhole for forced repetition. With n labelled objects, combinations count unordered subsets and permutations count ordered selections. Repeated objects reduce distinct arrangements.

## Concepts in depth

Add counts over disjoint alternatives and multiply counts across compatible stages. Use inclusion–exclusion when sets overlap, and complement counts to remove forbidden choices. Identical items require care about whether assigning labels changes the outcome.

## How to apply it

For nonnegative solutions to x+y+z=8, stars and bars gives C(10,2)=45. Requiring x≥2 means subtract 2 from x first, yielding C(8,2)=28. For bounded variables use inclusion–exclusion on violations.

## Worked example

Among 13 people, at least two share a birth month because there are only 12 possible months.

## Common mistake

In inclusion–exclusion, the overlap subtracted must count the same objects twice counted in the individual totals.

## Practice

Of 50 people, 20 know A, 18 know B and 7 know both. How many know at least one?

**Answer:** 31, by 20+18−7.
