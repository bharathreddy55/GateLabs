Read row labels, column labels, units and time windows before combining values. Identify whether cells are totals, averages, rates or indexes. Use the correct base when a question asks for a change or a contribution to a total.

## Concepts in depth

Read headers, units and the reference period before pulling numbers. Distinguish category totals from subtotals, and actual figures from targets or rates. A combined rate weights row rates by their denominators.

## How to apply it

Two units finish 9/10 and 40/50 of their tasks. The combined rate is 49/60≈81.7%, not the unweighted 85%. If a table already contains totals, avoid counting them again when summing the component rows.

## Worked example

A table shows Zone A output rising from 60 to 75 tonnes. The increase is 15 tonnes, and percentage increase is 15/60 = 25%.

## Common mistake

Averages across groups may require weights. A simple mean can mislead when group sizes differ.

## Practice

Region A contributed 30 units out of 120 across all regions. What was its share?

**Answer:** 25%, using the total across all regions as the denominator.
