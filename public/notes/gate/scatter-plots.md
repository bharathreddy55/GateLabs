Each point represents paired measured values. Identify direction, shape, strength and outliers. A rising cloud suggests positive association, while a falling cloud suggests negative association. Curved patterns may require more than a straight-line description.

## Concepts in depth

Every point is a paired observation. Check each axis scale and units before reading a point. A positive or negative association describes an overall trend, with possible exceptions. A plotted association alone provides no causal conclusion.

## How to apply it

A high-volume station with unusually little delay is an outlier relative to a general rising trend. Removing a point may change the apparent strength or direction, so compare fits carefully and explain why exclusion would be justified.

## Worked example

If delay tends to rise with rainfall but two high-rain observations have short delays, the bulk pattern can still be positive. Check whether those two cases are genuine or measurement errors.

## Common mistake

An observed correlation does not establish causation. Hidden variables can affect both measurements.

## Practice

What does a tight downward-sloping cloud suggest?

**Answer:** A strong negative linear association in the observed data.
