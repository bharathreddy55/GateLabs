A proposition is true or false. Implication p→q equals ¬p∨q and fails only when p is true and q is false. Its contrapositive is equivalent. De Morgan's laws exchange AND and OR under negation. Test equivalence or entailment by truth table, or transform each formula into a normal form.

## Concepts in depth

Implication P→Q fails only when P is true and Q false. De Morgan's laws change AND to OR under negation and negate both terms. Tautologies hold under all assignments, while satisfiability needs only one true assignment.

## How to apply it

To test whether P→Q equals Q→P, try P=false and Q=true. The first holds and the second fails. The equivalent contrapositive is ¬Q→¬P. For n variables a complete truth table has 2ⁿ rows.

## Worked example

The premises p→q and p imply q by modus ponens. The premises p→q and q alone leave p undecided.

## Common mistake

The converse q→p is not the contrapositive ¬q→¬p.

## Practice

Negate p∧q.

**Answer:** ¬p∨¬q, by De Morgan's law.
