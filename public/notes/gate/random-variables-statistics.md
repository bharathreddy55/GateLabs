A random variable maps outcomes to numbers. Its distribution assigns mass or density. Expected value is a weighted sum or integral. Variance = E[X²]−E[X]², with standard deviation its square root. Mean, median and mode respectively represent arithmetic center, middle rank and most frequent value.

## Concepts in depth

Expectation is linear, irrespective of independence. For discrete X, weight each value by its probability. For continuous X, integrate against the density. Variance is the second moment minus squared mean. Covariance contributes to the variance of a sum.

## How to apply it

A Bernoulli(p) variable has E[X]=p and E[X²]=p because X²=X. Its variance is p(1−p). Independent variables have zero covariance, but zero covariance alone does not imply independence.

## Worked example

For X=0 or 2 with equal probability, E[X]=1 and E[X²]=2, so variance is 1. Shifting both values by 7 changes the mean, while variance remains 1.

## Common mistake

E[X²] and E[X]² are generally different. Use each in its intended place.

## Practice

If Y=3X+2, how does standard deviation change?

**Answer:** It becomes three times X's standard deviation. Adding 2 shifts location only.
