Tense locates events in time and aspect presents their course or relation to another time. Progressive uses a form of be plus -ing. Perfect uses have plus a past participle. In a transitive passive, the active object becomes subject, and a form of be plus the past participle preserves the tense and aspect as appropriate.

## Concepts in depth

Tense locates a situation and aspect describes its relation to a reference time. Present perfect connects a past event to the present. Past perfect places one event before another past reference. Continuous aspect typically presents an ongoing action. In a passive construction, be carries the original tense and the main verb becomes a past participle.

## How to apply it

Compare “She submitted yesterday” with “She has submitted already.” A finished-time expression like yesterday normally goes with the simple past. In “They are reviewing the files,” keep the ongoing aspect with “The files are being reviewed.” Intransitive verbs like arrive do not take ordinary object-to-subject passives.

## Worked example

'The children planted a tree' becomes 'A tree was planted by the children'. The past tense is carried by was.

## Common mistake

Do not change the event time when changing voice. In a passive, the agent phrase can be omitted if it is unknown or irrelevant.

## Practice

Which choice describes an ongoing action at a past time, 'I read' or 'I was reading'?

**Answer:** I was reading, the past progressive.
