An indefinite integral gives antiderivatives plus a constant. A definite integral accumulates signed area, and the fundamental theorem evaluates it using an antiderivative under ordinary continuity assumptions. Substitution reverses the chain rule, and integration by parts reverses the product rule. Change the limits when substituting in a definite integral.

## Concepts in depth

Indefinite integration gives antiderivatives plus a constant. Definite integration accumulates signed area and uses the upper antiderivative value less the lower one. Substitution changes both integrand and limits. Integration by parts reverses the product rule.

## How to apply it

For ∫₀¹ 2x/(1+x²) dx, set u=1+x² and integrate 1/u from 1 to 2 to get ln 2. Geometric area of a curve below the axis requires taking the magnitude on that part.

## Worked example

Integrate 2x(x²+1)³ from 0 to 1. Set u=x²+1, so du=2x dx and bounds become 1 to 2. The value is (2⁴−1⁴)/4=15/4.

## Common mistake

An integral with regions below the axis gives signed area. Geometric area needs their magnitudes.

## Practice

Find the definite integral of 3x² from 1 to 2.

**Answer:** 7, from x³ evaluated at the two endpoints.
