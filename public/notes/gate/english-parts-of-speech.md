Classify words by their function in context. Nouns name, verbs express an action or state, adjectives describe nouns and adverbs can modify verbs, adjectives or other adverbs. Use a or an with an indefinite singular count noun according to the following sound. The normally marks a specific identifiable referent. Find the head subject to establish agreement.

## Concepts in depth

Classify words by what they do. Nouns can head subjects or objects. Verbs carry tense or take an auxiliary. Adjectives modify nouns and can follow linking verbs, as in “the plan seems sound.” Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, other adverbs, or the clause. “Light” is a noun in “the light went out,” a verb in “light the lamp,” and an adjective in “a light bag.” Use neighbouring words as evidence instead of guessing from the ending. A preposition heads a phrase such as “after lunch,” while a subordinating conjunction can introduce a clause such as “after we ate.”

## How to apply it

Use a/an for one nonspecific countable item. The next spoken sound determines the form. Use “a university” and “an MBA”. Use the when the reference is identifiable, as in “I bought a book. The book is on my desk.” Compare general “Water freezes” with specific “The water in this pipe froze.” For agreement, bracket intervening phrases as in “The cost [of the repairs] has risen” and “The repairs [to the bridge] have ended.” Compare “A number of candidates are waiting” with “The number of candidates is rising.” In the first, a number of means several. In the second, the subject names a single total.

## Worked example

'An hour' uses an because hour begins with a vowel sound. In 'The list of names is long', list is the head subject, so the verb is is.

## Common mistake

A word can change function. 'Fast' is an adjective in 'a fast train' and an adverb in 'runs fast'.

## Practice

Choose: The box of books is/are heavy.

**Answer:** Is, because box is singular and of books is a dependent phrase.
