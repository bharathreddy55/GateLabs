Identify introductions, references and logical connectors. An opening sentence often introduces a topic before a later sentence uses a definite noun phrase or pronoun. Pair evidence with the claim it supports and check the logic of the complete sequence.

## Concepts in depth

A good opener introduces the topic without requiring an unnamed pronoun or earlier event. “This result,” “the second method,” and “therefore” normally refer backwards. Pair strong references before deciding the full order.

## How to apply it

An order might be problem → proposed method → observations → consequence. A sentence introducing two methods should precede one naming “the latter.” When two sequences look plausible, read them aloud and check every reference, not just the first sentence.

## Worked example

A: A bank opened a mobile branch. B: The branch serves several remote villages. C: This reduced travel time for customers. The dependency chain gives A–B–C.

## Common mistake

A repeated word can mislead. A pronoun or an explicit result often provides stronger evidence about the order.

## Practice

Which should come first: 'These outlets extended service hours' or 'The bank opened three outlets'?

**Answer:** The opening of the three outlets supplies the referent for these outlets.
