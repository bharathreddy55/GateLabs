Translation shifts each point by the same vector. Rotation preserves distances and turns about a center. Reflection reverses orientation across an axis or plane. Scaling changes lengths by its factor and changes area and volume by its square and cube. For folding and cutting problems, track layers, fold lines and mirrored positions before unfolding. In assemblies, label corresponding faces, edges or pieces and use invariants such as adjacency and orientation.

## Concepts in depth

Rotations preserve handedness, reflections reverse it, and translations preserve orientation and relative positions. Pick an off-centre marker or corner to distinguish operations on otherwise symmetric figures. On coordinates, a quarter-turn anticlockwise sends (x,y) to (−y,x), while reflection in the y-axis sends (x,y) to (−x,y).

## How to apply it

An L-shape with a dot on one end can be tracked by moving the corner and dot together. A mirror image may match the length and area of a rotated original while the dot lies on the opposite side of the reference arm.

## Worked example

Rotate (2,1) 90° anticlockwise around the origin to get (−1,2). Reflect the result across the y-axis to get (1,2). The order of the operations matters.

## Common mistake

A hole through a folded stack appears at every reflected position on unfolding, except when a position lies exactly on a fold.

## Practice

Scaling a cube's side by a factor of three multiplies its volume by what?

**Answer:** 27, since all three dimensions are multiplied by three.
