Look for extrema at critical points, nondifferentiable points and endpoints of the allowed interval. A sign change of f' identifies increasing-to-decreasing or decreasing-to-increasing behaviour. The mean value theorem requires continuity on [a,b] and differentiability on (a,b), yielding a point c with slope equal to the secant slope.

## Concepts in depth

On a closed interval a continuous function attains extrema. Candidates include endpoints and interior points where derivative is zero or undefined. The mean value theorem also needs differentiability in the open interval and equates an interior derivative to the secant slope.

## How to apply it

For x²−4x on [0,3], evaluate x=0, x=2 and x=3 to compare 0, −4 and −3. The minimum is −4. A stationary point by itself need not be an extremum, as x³ at zero shows.

## Worked example

For f(x)=x² on [1,3], the secant slope is (9−1)/2=4. The derivative is 2x, so c=2 meets the theorem.

## Common mistake

Check endpoints for absolute extrema even when f'=0 has no solution inside the interval.

## Practice

Find the minimum of x²−6x+10 on the real line.

**Answer:** 1 at x=3, by completing the square as (x−3)²+1.
