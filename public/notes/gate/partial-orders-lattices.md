A partial order is reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive. A Hasse diagram omits self and transitive edges. A least element is below every element, while a minimal element only has nothing strictly below it. A lattice has a unique meet and join for every pair, given by greatest lower and least upper bounds.

## Concepts in depth

A partial order is reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive. A Hasse diagram omits reflexive edges and edges implied by transitivity. Least upper and greatest lower bounds may exist even when the two elements are incomparable.

## How to apply it

In divisors of 12 ordered by divisibility, meet(4,6)=2 and join(4,6)=12. They are gcd and lcm. A lattice needs a meet and join for every pair, not a total order between pairs.

## Worked example

Positive divisors of 12 ordered by divisibility form a lattice. Meet(4,6)=gcd(4,6)=2 and join(4,6)=lcm(4,6)=12.

## Common mistake

Antisymmetry permits equality. Distinguish it from asymmetry.

## Practice

Under inclusion, what are meet and join of subsets A and B of a fixed set?

**Answer:** A∩B and A∪B respectively.
