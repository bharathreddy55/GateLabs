Connectivity asks whether paths join vertices. A tree is connected and acyclic, with n−1 edges. A matching shares no endpoints, and a perfect matching covers every vertex. In a bipartite graph, Hall's condition for saturating one side requires every subset there to have at least as many distinct neighbours. Proper vertex colouring gives different colours to adjacent vertices. Every bipartite nonempty graph with an edge uses two colours; an odd cycle requires at least three.

## Concepts in depth

A bridge increases the number of components if removed. A matching uses edges with no shared endpoints. In a bipartite graph a matching of the whole left side exists exactly when every subset of that side has at least as many neighbours as members. A graph is 2-colourable exactly when it has no odd cycle.

## How to apply it

A connected tree with n≥2 has n−1 edges and can be 2-coloured by parity of depth. A triangle needs three colours. A vertex having a neighbour alone does not ensure it can be matched if two vertices compete for the same one.

## Worked example

A 5-cycle is connected and has maximum matching size two. Alternating two colours fails at the last edge, so three colours are needed.

## Common mistake

A maximal matching cannot accept another edge directly. A maximum matching has the greatest possible cardinality.

## Practice

How many edges does a tree on 12 vertices have?

**Answer:** 11. In a tree, each added vertex contributes one connection without creating a cycle.
