Use a diagram or grid with one slot for each person, time or condition. Write fixed placements, then connect relative clues. Keep a list of possible values when a clue offers alternatives. Eliminate a case the moment it conflicts with a placed fact.

## Concepts in depth

Represent options with a grid, slots or a table. Fill fixed positions and merge adjacency blocks before applying inequalities or negations. When a clue gives two branches, keep both until a contradiction rules one out.

## How to apply it

In a five-member ranking, “A above B, B above C” fixes relative order but not absolute positions. Adding “one person sits between A and B” constrains a gap. Keep relative and absolute information separate to avoid forcing a position too early.

## Worked example

Three seats are numbered left to right. B is in the middle and C sits right of A. The remaining order is A–B–C.

## Common mistake

If a clue specifies left and right from a person's perspective, orient the diagram accordingly.

## Practice

D and E are side by side and F is in the middle of three seats. Can both clues hold?

**Answer:** No. The end seats available to D and E are not adjacent.
