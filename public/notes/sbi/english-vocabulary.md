Synonyms share meaning in some contexts, antonyms contrast, and homophones sound alike. Homonyms can share form or sound while differing in meaning. ## Concepts in depth

Use prefixes and roots to estimate meaning, then check function and register. The suffix -able often marks an adjective expressing possibility, while -tion often marks a noun. Synonyms can have different strength or collocation. A plausible dictionary meaning can still be wrong for the sentence.

## How to apply it

In “The officer's response was measured,” tone suggests controlled or careful. The sense “recorded in units” fits a quantity but not this context. For a blank, first predict your own neutral word, then compare the options for grammar and tone.

## Worked example

The noun 'decision' relates to the verb 'decide'. English usually says 'make a decision', illustrating a collocation. In 'the wind whispered', the nonhuman wind receives a human action, illustrating personification.

## Common mistake

An idiom generally has a conventional meaning. The words in 'give up' as a phrasal verb can mean quit rather than physically give something.

## Practice

Which pair are homophones: flour/flower or slow/quick?

**Answer:** Flour and flower sound alike and differ in spelling and meaning.
