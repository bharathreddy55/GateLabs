Write the slots in their real order, such as top to bottom or Monday through Friday. Place fixed clues first, then translate gaps into distances on the diagram. For two attributes, mark a grid by person and slot and eliminate incompatible pairs. Explore only unresolved cases.

## Concepts in depth

Number floors from a known base and put weekdays or dates in chronological order. Interpret “two between” as a gap of three indexes. Mark immediate adjacency separately from “above” or “later,” which permit empty intervening positions.

## How to apply it

If A stays two floors above B on floors 1 through 5, possible pairs begin (3,1), (4,2) and (5,3). A clue excluding the top floor rules out only the last pair. Keep the remaining possibilities until other clues distinguish them.

## Worked example

A is two floors above B in a four-floor building. Possible pairs are (A3,B1) and (A4,B2). If B is on an even floor, only (A4,B2) remains.

## Common mistake

'Two floors above' usually differs from 'two people between' because the latter leaves a gap of three positions.

## Practice

If P is before Q with exactly one slot between, can Q be two slots after P?

**Answer:** Yes. Positions 1 and 3 have exactly one slot between them.
