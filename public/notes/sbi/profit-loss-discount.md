Profit equals selling price minus cost price. Profit and loss percentages normally use cost as their base. A discount applies to marked price. For multiple discounts, multiply the remaining fractions instead of adding rates.

## Concepts in depth

Profit and loss percentages usually use cost price. Discount uses marked price. For successive discounts, multiply retained fractions. Separate selling price from net receipt if a commission or tax is separately specified.

## How to apply it

A ₹1,000 item marked 25% above cost is marked ₹1,250. A 12% discount makes selling price ₹1,100 and profit 10% of cost. Treating 25−12 as the profit percentage uses mismatched bases.

## Worked example

An item costing ₹800 is marked ₹1,000 and discounted 10%. It sells for ₹900, so profit is ₹100 or 12.5% of cost.

## Common mistake

Discount percentage and profit percentage use different bases.

## Practice

A seller gives 20% off ₹600. What is the price?

**Answer:** ₹480, or 0.80 × 600.
