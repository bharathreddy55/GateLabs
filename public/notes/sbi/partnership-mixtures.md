For profit allocated by capital and time, use capital-month products as weights. For a mixture that conserves an amount such as solute, combine the quantities of that component and divide by total volume. Track additions and removals before recomputing concentration.

## Concepts in depth

In a simple partnership, profit shares follow capital weighted by duration, unless the terms say otherwise. Mixtures balance the amount of the constituent, not just its percentage. If liquid is well stirred, a withdrawal removes constituents in the current proportion.

## How to apply it

Mix 20 L at 30% and 10 L at 60% to get (6+6)/30=40%. In replacement problems, the original constituent fraction remaining after each equal withdrawal and refill with water is multiplied by 1−withdrawal volume/tank volume.

## Worked example

Mix 2 L at 20% and 3 L at 40%. Solute is 0.4+1.2=1.6 L in 5 L, giving 32%.

## Common mistake

A plain average of rates is appropriate only when the quantities being weighted are equal.

## Practice

A invests ₹2,000 for 6 months and B ₹3,000 for 4 months. What is their profit-sharing ratio under capital × time?

**Answer:** 1:1, since both contribute 12,000 rupee-months.
