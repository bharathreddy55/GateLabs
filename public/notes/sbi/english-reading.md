Skim for text purpose and organization, scan for a requested fact, and reread the surrounding sentences to infer meaning. Distinguish a direct statement, a warranted inference and a personal response. Check pronoun antecedents and cohesive links across sentences.

## Concepts in depth

A passage normally separates its central claim from reasons, examples, concessions and qualifications. A main-idea answer should cover the passage's scope and its point. A detail answer needs direct textual support. An inference can be unstated if the evidence warrants it.

## How to apply it

Mark the shift after however or yet, then paraphrase the author's point in one sentence. An example about declining bus use after a fare increase supports a limited association. It does not prove fares explain every change in ridership. Reject options that introduce new causes or turn some into all.

## Worked example

In 'The stream rose after rain. It crossed the path by dusk', it refers to the stream. The two sentences also give a time sequence.

## Common mistake

Choose the central claim of the whole passage for a title question, rather than one memorable detail.

## Practice

Why might an author use a specific example after a general claim?

**Answer:** To illustrate or support the claim in a situation the reader can inspect.
