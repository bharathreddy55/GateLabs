## Concepts in depth

Convert any coded symbols into ordinary inequalities first. Transitivity works along a consistently directed chain. Strictness carries through if at least one comparison is strict. Opposing directions around a shared middle element may leave the endpoints incomparable.

## How to apply it

A≥B>C forces A>C. A>B and C>B alone leave A and C unordered. Equality lets you substitute directly. If asked which conclusion definitely follows, do not pick a comparison that merely can be true.

## Worked example

A ≥ B and B > C imply A > C. But A > B and C > B do not order A and C.

## Common mistake

Check the direction before chaining. A shared smaller or larger middle element does not order the endpoints.

## Practice

If P ≤ Q = R < S, is P < S certain?

**Answer:** Yes. P is no greater than R, and R is strictly below S.
